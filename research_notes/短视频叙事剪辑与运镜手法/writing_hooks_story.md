# Short-video scriptwriting for knowledge/explainer content: hooks, story structure, pacing, retention (subtitle-only, no narration, ~2 min landscape, CN 18–25)

Research date: 2026-10-04. About 25 tool calls. **Overall caveat on sources:** most Chinese "creator-economy" writing on hooks and 完播率 is SEO and course-marketing material (帆软 blog, 人人都是产品经理, 数英). It repeats the same rules of thumb and gives **no primary platform numbers**. I found no official 抖音创作者中心 or 巨量引擎 page that publishes benchmark values for 2s跳出率 or 5s完播率. The hard numbers below come mostly from English-language vendor analyses (OpusClip, OutlierKit) and the leaked MrBeast production doc. Treat them as directional. Each item is labelled evidence, vendor claim or folklore.

---

## Q1. Hooks in the first 1–3 s and 5–8 s; what is known about 3 s / 5 s retention, 完播率, 2 s bounce

### Takeaway
The first 3 s decide whether a viewer stays. That is the one point every source agrees on. A good hook keeps roughly 80–90 % of viewers past second 3 (vendor data), and the rest of the video then loses viewers gradually. For a ~2-min piece the "hook" really has two parts. Seconds 0–3 must stop the swipe: one surprising image plus one line, a 反常识 claim or the result shown first. Seconds 3–10 must prove the claim with one concrete example and say what the viewer gets. Greetings, background and tables of contents in the opening are reliably harmful.

### Cited Findings
- **"黄金三秒"**: viewers decide within 3–7 s whether to keep watching. Recommended remedy: plant tension points (反转, 悬念, 惊喜) through the video to lift 完播率. — [36氪 (2023 short-video script guide)](https://www.36kr.com/p/2043528993655809)
- Chinese practitioner hook toolkit for the first 3 s: (a) **state the payoff up front**, i.e. 告诉用户"看完这条视频你能得到什么"; (b) **break the fourth wall** with lines like "别划走"; (c) **recognisable viral sound effects** that set the viewer's expectation. 完播率 is described as a major factor in algorithmic distribution. — [数英 Digitaling](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/374108.html)
- **Vendor data (OpusClip, 500 videos, undated, no peer review):** strong hooks keep **80–90 % of viewers through the first 3 s**, followed by a gradual decline. Average completion is **62 % for 21–34 s videos** and **48 % for 60 s+ videos**. Videos at **70 % average watch time got 4.3× the impressions** of videos at 40 %. Accurate captions gave **+12 % retention**. Videos with a **"pattern interrupt" every ~4 s averaged 58 % retention vs 41 %** for static talking heads. — [OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data)
- **YouTube-derived hook template for the first 30 s** (OutlierKit), in three 10-s parts: **0–10 s "prove the title with one real example"**, **10–20 s "say why it matters to the viewer"**, **20–30 s a one-line roadmap that repeats any number from the title**. Openings to avoid: greetings, backstory, detailed tables of contents, and saving the best for last. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **MrBeast production manual (leaked Sept 2024):** "The first minute… shows that the thumbnail meets the expectations." It calls for an **"element of surprise"** within roughly the first 30 s, e.g. a crane lowering a house into the ground. The principle is to deliver the promise of the cover and title immediately. — [GIGAZINE summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production); also [OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/mrbeast-doc-pdf-leak), [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- 毕导 uses an absurd or crude topic as the hook itself, e.g. a video on how to "elegantly eat 撒尿牛肉丸". The odd premise creates curiosity before any science appears. — [36氪 on knowledge creators](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725129342977)
- The 帆软 blog defines 完播率 only conceptually ("观众观看完作品的比例… 评估作品质量和用户粘性的关键指标"). It gives **no numeric benchmark** and no official definition of 2s跳出率 or 5s完播率. — [帆软 FineReport blog](https://www.fanruan.com/blog/article/1248513/)

### Inferences
- **Hook types ranked for a subtitle-only, music-only, 3D/2D animated video.** No voice means the image and the first line must carry everything.
  1. **结果前置 / 反常识 visual + claim**: show the strange outcome first, e.g. a number that seems impossible next to a model. This is the closest analogue to MrBeast's "house into the ground" and to OutlierKit's "prove the title with one real example". Strongest choice.
  2. **数字冲击**: one big number at a large font size, with a physical-scale comparison in 3D. It reads in under 1 s.
  3. **提问 / 悬念**: works only if the question is concrete and personal ("你每天…其实…?"). Abstract questions such as "什么是X?" are weak.
  4. **冲突**: two things that "shouldn't" coexist.
  The hook line should be readable in ≤1.5 s, so ≤8–10 字 (see Q4 reading speeds).
- **Seconds 3–8 must pay off the first micro-promise.** Show one concrete example or evidence frame so the viewer sees the claim is real. Then open the bigger loop ("但真正离谱的是…"). This is the OutlierKit 0–10 s step compressed to short-video timing.
- **Never open on a logo, title card, black screen or slow establishing shot.** With no narration, a quiet 2 s of music over an empty scene works like "Hey guys, welcome back". It is the most likely cause of 2-s swipes.
- "别划走"-style fourth-wall lines are common on 抖音. For an 18–25 male B站 audience, a cliché line may signal low quality. Prefer a claim over a plea. *(Opinion, not tested.)*

### Gaps
- **No official or verifiable 抖音, B站 or 视频号 benchmark numbers were found** for 2s跳出率, 5s完播率 or "good" 完播率 by duration or vertical. Widely repeated figures ("5秒完播率要>50 %", "完播率 30 % 才进下一个流量池") could not be traced to a primary source. Treat them as **folklore**. Search results pointing to bilibili cv45263020 and toutiao returned no readable content.
- No data specific to Chinese 18–25 men vs other demographics.
- The OpusClip numbers are TikTok/global, undated and proprietary.

## Q2. Story structures for 1–3 min knowledge videos, with beat timings

### Takeaway
All the structures share one skeleton. Make a concrete, surprising promise. Open a loop (a question the viewer needs answered). Pay it off in layers, each layer opening the next loop. Put the biggest surprise about two-thirds of the way in, not at the very end. Finish with a short 升华: a new perspective on everyday life plus a question. Re-hook rhythm for a 2-min piece: a new mini-surprise every ~15–25 s and a visual change every ~4–10 s.

### Cited Findings
- Recommended formula for educational content: **"提出问题 + 解决方案 + 展示总结"**. Also: end with **theme elevation (升华) or a call to engagement**. — [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/2043528993655809)
- **钩子 doctrine:** keep people watching to the end by embedding **反转** (plot reversals; the cited example is 朱一旦's Rolex), **悬念** (unanswered questions) and **彩蛋** (a preview of hidden content that rewards finishing). — [数英](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/374108.html)
- **Open loops / re-hooks:** "Every 2 to 3 minutes, tease the next part before you show it", e.g. "The next one is the one I still cannot believe." These carry viewers through transitions where drop-off happens. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Payoff placement:** put the strongest moment **about two-thirds of the way through**, because "Most people never reach the end. Saving the best for last means most viewers never see it." — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Worked example:** the video "23 Geography Facts That Sound Wrong" (channel Price of Travel, 19K subs, 496K views, 22.9× channel norm) opens with one surprising fact (Reno is west of Los Angeles). It then says why it matters, states the full count as a roadmap, and **delivers a new surprise every 20–30 s**. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **MrBeast manual:** the content proper starts around 1:00 after the hook-and-confirm minute. **Re-engagement beats are placed at ~3 and ~6 min**. — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)
- **星球研究所 "目录式" structure:** a big theme is split layer by layer along time scales ("6500万年的华夏山水 / 1万年的文明史诗 / 100年的重塑山河"), then into regions, cities and specific projects. The editor describes this as "一层一层往下拆". — [澎湃 (interview with 主编魏桢)](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_22683393)
- **Kurzgesagt:** scripts balance fact against narrative and take **"about a dozen drafts"**. The goal is to "offer a new perspective on a topic and leave viewers with new insights – without them really noticing." — [Kurzgesagt "Our video process"](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/)

### Inferences: suggested beat sheet for a ~120 s subtitle-only knowledge film
This compresses the cited patterns. The timings are a design proposal, not measured optima.

| t (s) | Beat | Purpose / device |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **钩子**: 反常识 / 结果前置 image + ≤10-字 line | Stop the swipe |
| 3–10 | **证据**: one concrete example proving the hook is real; open the main loop ("为什么?") | Confirm the promise (MrBeast "thumbnail meets expectations") |
| 10–25 | **冲突 / stakes**: who or what is affected; why it should be impossible | Make it matter to "you" (OutlierKit 10–20 s) |
| 25–45 | **第一层揭晓** + new question | First payoff; re-hook ("但这还不是最离谱的") |
| 45–70 | **第二层**: escalation / data scene | Mini-surprise every ~20 s |
| 70–85 | **反转 / big reveal** at ≈ 2/3 | Strongest moment at ~60–65 % of runtime |
| 85–105 | **升华**: back to the viewer's own life; a new perspective | Kurzgesagt-style "new insight without noticing" |
| 105–120 | **金句 + 评论区问题 / callback to the opening image** | Comments, shares, rewatch loop |

- Map this onto 起承转合: 起 = 0–25, 承 = 25–70, 转 = 70–85, 合 = 85–120. The "黄金三秒→冲突→悬念→反转→升华" formula is the same skeleton.
- **钩子密度:** at 2 min, "every 2–3 minutes" (YouTube) is too sparse. The 20–30 s surprise cadence from the geography example is the better-supported reference. Re-hooks every **15–25 s** is a reasonable target, which gives about 5–6 re-hooks in 120 s. The commonly quoted "every 10–15 s" is **folklore**, with no primary source found.

### Gaps
- No primary data (retention curves) for Chinese knowledge videos showing where drop-offs cluster at 1–3 min lengths.
- Could not obtain creator-side retention graphs from B站 creator docs (B站创作中心 / UP主学院 were not reachable in the searches).

## Q3. Making facts emotional: protagonists, stakes, specific detail, data as story, and how named creators do it

### Takeaway
People share because of emotion, not logic. 星球研究所's editor says so directly, and that is the clearest primary statement found. The working techniques are:
- a concrete protagonist or hypothetical person (罗翔's 张三, 半佛's escaped pig);
- absurd or comic framing laid over rigorous content (毕导);
- explanation through visual mechanism rather than words (回形针);
- splitting a huge subject into nested, time-scaled layers (星球研究所).

A warning sign: viewers remember the meme (张三) more than the knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- 星球研究所 editor 魏桢 names the four core elements as **"专业、审美、视野、情感"** and says **"读者不太会因为逻辑转发，但会因为情感来认同观点"**. For video specifically, the content has to be simplified and **post-production is 70–80 % of the workload**. — [澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_22683393)
- **毕导:** absurd topics and humour make niche science shareable. In the 撒尿牛肉丸 video he set a playful challenge mid-video: viewers who "can't aim" type "1" in the comments, others solve lim sin x / x. This mixes crude comedy with calculus and **explicitly drives comments**. — [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725129342977)
- **半佛仙人** uses self-aware, colloquial language and absurd hypotheticals, e.g. explaining wildlife classification through escaped farm pigs eating crops. **回形针** turns complex mechanisms into animation instead of verbal description. — [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725129342977)
- **罗翔 critique:** viewers remember the **"张三" meme** more than the legal concepts. A 9:32 video on abolishing the death penalty got 3M+ views, yet "看过罗翔老师视频的人很多很多，真的学到了很多法律知识的人却没几个". Early winners 巫师财经 and 半佛仙人 "吃到第一波红利". The article also criticises many knowledge videos as "articles converted to video + memes + an authoritative tone". — [人人都是产品经理](https://www.woshipm.com/it/5135852.html)
- **Spoken-style principle (for scripts):** colloquial over jargon, **short sentences to cut cognitive load**, and a signature catchphrase for brand recall, e.g. 朱一旦's "有钱人的生活，往往就是这么朴实无华且枯燥". — [数英](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/374108.html)
- **Kurzgesagt:** reshape facts into a narrative that gives a new perspective "without them really noticing". — [Kurzgesagt](https://kurzgesagt.org/youtube/)

### Inferences
- **Give every data scene a 张三**, a recurring nameless character (2D flat figure) whom the facts happen *to*. A protagonist plus stakes turns "X% of Y" into "this person loses Z". 罗翔's success shows a recurring character becomes the brand. His critique shows the knowledge still has to be anchored to that character's fate, or only the meme survives.
- **Turn numbers into physical scale in 3D.** One number per scene, compared to something the viewer owns or knows (a phone, a dorm, a 地铁 carriage). This is the 回形针 principle of "show the mechanism, don't describe it", and it suits a no-narration format especially well.
- **Humour as a pressure valve.** 毕导 and 半佛 alternate rigorous content with absurd jokes. In subtitle-only form, a deadpan short line after a big reveal ("嗯。应该没事吧。") acts as the release beat.
- **Name a specific human detail** (a year, a place, a price, an object) rather than a category. This is standard narrative craft and is consistent with the "专业 + 情感" pairing above, but no source in this session quantifies it.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve first-person writing-process interviews with 小Lin说, 李永乐, 无穷小亮 or 回形针's founders. Searches returned unrelated journalism or sci-fi interviews. 小Lin说's method (whiteboard plus a running analogy, "说人话") is widely discussed but **unsourced here**.
- No quantitative evidence that protagonist-based framing raises 完播率 on Chinese platforms.

## Q4. On-screen text without voice-over: line length, reading speed, lines per scene, 金句, endings and comment prompts

### Takeaway
Chinese subtitle standards cap a line at about 15–16 characters and give at least 1 s (max ~6 s) per subtitle. In this format the text is the only narrative channel and has to compete with animation for the eyes. So write far shorter than the maximum: about **6–12 字 per line, 1 line per beat (2 at most)**, held for about **(chars ÷ 5) + 0.5 s**. Endings should combine a 金句 with a concrete question or task that viewers can answer in the comments (the 毕导 "type 1" model).

### Cited Findings
- **Chinese subtitle line length: max 15 characters per line** (citing City University of Hong Kong, Dept. of Translation & Linguistics). **Minimum 1 s, maximum 6 s on screen**, plus about 0.5 s after the speech ends. Font size per BBC guidance: at least 44 px on HD; screen height ÷ 20 to ÷ 10, i.e. **54–108 px at 1080p**. — [Subanana 2026 guide](https://subanana.com/blog/four-points-to-note-when-creating-video-subtitle/)
- Netflix publishes a dedicated **Simplified Chinese (PRC) Timed Text Style Guide**. — [Netflix Partner Help](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/215986007-Chinese-Simplified-Timed-Text-Style-Guide). *The page was located but could not be fetched. The commonly cited values, **16 characters per line, 2 lines max, ~9 characters/s for adult content**, come from prior knowledge and were **not verified in this session**.*
- A 2026 eye-tracking study tested subtitle speed effects for Chinese viewers: Jia, Szarkowska & Díaz-Cintas, *Perspectives* (DOI 10.1080/0907676X.2026.2686722). — [UA observatorio record](https://observatorio-cientifico.ua.es/documentos/6a651af5e349fb07c9bfcc66). *Only the metadata was accessible; speeds tested and findings unknown.*
- Captions alone were associated with **+12 % retention** (vendor claim). — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data)
- **Comment-driving device:** 毕导 asks viewers to respond with a low-effort token ("1") or solve a tiny problem. — [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/1725129342977)
- **Catchphrase / 金句 as brand:** the 朱一旦 example. Short sentences reduce cognitive load. Read scripts aloud to test rhythm. — [数英](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/374108.html)
- **彩蛋** (a promised hidden payoff) rewards finishing the video. — [数英](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/374108.html)

### Inferences: writing rules for subtitle-only text
- **Speed:** the brief's assumption of ~4–6 字/s is conservative compared with subtitle norms (~9 cps if the Netflix value is right). That conservatism is appropriate here, because viewers must also parse 3D animation and the text *is* the story. Use **5 字/s + 0.5 s** as the default hold: an 8-字 line gets about 2.1 s, a 12-字 line about 2.9 s. Hold key 金句 and numbers longer (≥2.5 s) and give them a beat of silence or a musical hit.
- **Line length:** a hard ceiling of 15–16 字 per line (standard). Target 6–12. Prefer **one line on screen at a time**. If two lines are needed, split them at a meaning boundary and reveal the second on the beat.
- **Lines per scene:** with about 10–20 s per scene, that means roughly 3–6 subtitle events per scene. More than that and the scene becomes a slideshow.
- **Phrasing:** spoken register, not article prose (the 罗翔 / "article converted to video" critique). Drop 的/了/是 where the meaning survives. One idea per line. Keep the number and the noun together on one line.
- **金句:** ≤12 字, parallel or contrastive structure ("不是X，是Y"), placed at the 升华 beat and echoed in the cover or title.
- **Ending:** (1) a callback to the opening image (rewatch loop); (2) a **specific, answerable** comment prompt such as "你猜…?" / "你身边有没有…?" / "评论区扣1", not a generic "你怎么看?"; (3) optionally a series tease for the next episode. Each is supported in principle by the 毕导 and 彩蛋 examples. The causal effect on share rate is **not quantified**.

### Gaps
- Not verified: the Netflix SC cps values, and the results of the 2026 Chinese subtitle-speed eye-tracking study.
- No data on optimal subtitle speed when there is **no audio speech at all**. Viewers then read without auditory support, which may lower comfortable speed. Reasoned, not measured.
- No platform data comparing comment prompts by type and the resulting comment rate.

## Q5. Pacing: rate of on-screen change, tension and release, reveal placement, how long a breather can last

### Takeaway
Evidence points to **a visual change every ~4–10 s** (pattern interrupts) and **a narrative surprise every ~20–30 s**, with the biggest reveal at about two-thirds of runtime. A "breather" with no new information should be short. The sources imply that static stretches longer than ~5–10 s are risky on faceless videos. Music-only formats need visual motion even during rest beats.

### Cited Findings
- Pattern interrupts **every ~4 s → 58 % average retention vs 41 %** for static talking-head videos (vendor, 500-video sample). — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data)
- **Faceless videos:** "changing on-screen elements every 5–10 seconds" prevents departures during static imagery. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Surprise cadence of 20–30 s** in a 22.9×-outperforming list video. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Biggest payoff at about two-thirds of runtime.** — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Re-engagement beats** at fixed points (3 and 6 min in long-form). — [GIGAZINE / MrBeast doc](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)
- **Length vs completion trade-off:** "31 to 60 seconds often generates more total watch time" than shorter videos despite lower completion. A 40 s video at 60 % beats a 20 s video at 70 %. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data)
- For 星球研究所, video forces simplification compared with text, and post-production takes 70–80 % of effort. — [澎湃](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_22683393)

### Inferences
- **Three nested clocks for a 120 s film:**
  - Micro, every 2–5 s: something moves or changes. A camera move, a new element, a subtitle swap or a number ticking all count.
  - Meso, every 15–25 s: a scene or idea change, each ending on an open question.
  - Macro: hook (0–3 s) → reveal (~75–85 s) → 升华 / close.
- **Tension and release:** escalate over 2–3 meso beats, then release with a joke or deadpan line and 2–4 s of visual "air" (a slow push-in on the model, music swell, no new text). Keep breathers **≤4–5 s** and keep motion going during them. A full static frame plus no text for more than ~5 s is the riskiest moment in a subtitle-only video. This extrapolates from the 4 s / 5–10 s figures and is not directly measured.
- **Reveal placement:** around 70–85 s of 120 s, i.e. 60–70 % of runtime, then about 30 s of 升华 and ending. Do not put the reveal in the last 10 s.
- **Music sync:** with no voice, the music track is the pacing engine. Put hard visual changes and reveal lines on downbeats, and leave continuous motions on their own clock. (The repo has a separate edit-rhythm skill for this; no external source was checked here.)

### Gaps
- No Chinese-platform retention-curve data relating cut frequency to 完播率. The "画面每3秒一变" type rules that circulate in Chinese courses are **folklore**, with no primary source found.
- No evidence on how a single continuous music track (no voice) changes acceptable breather length.
- B站's landscape audience may tolerate slower pacing than 抖音's vertical feed. This is plausible but unsourced in this session.
