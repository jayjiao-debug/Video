## Story-worthy

**Five most surprising facts**
1. **Your brain is rewarded before the good part arrives.** The caudate works during the *anticipation* and the nucleus accumbens at the *peak*. Anticipating an abstract reward recruits "a pathway distinct from … the peak pleasure itself" (#4, #5). The waiting is part of the pleasure.
2. **Only 7 of 38 people** got goosebumps from the same famous passage, Mozart's "Tuba Mirum" (#16). Chills are personal. That sets up the self-test: "你是那 7 个人之一吗？"
3. **Scientists deleted the chills section of songs, and chills dropped** from 30 to 21 reports (N = 24). They did not vanish, and "the moments preceding" the section matter too (#19, #20). This is the published version of our film's experiment.
4. **The drop has a measurable recipe:** remove the bass, add a riser, bring everything back at once. Skin conductance is significantly higher at the drop than in the breakdown or build (#29, #30). The break routine "sets up, delays, and gratifies expectations".
5. **Turn dopamine up or down and musical pleasure follows** (#8, 27 people, double-blind). It is the same reward machinery as "food … and money" (#2, #5).
   Runner-up: chill moments carry a measurable loudness jump at 920–4400 Hz (#18), exactly the band a filter sweep opens.

**Strongest human story**
Salimpoor's search for the right brains (#6): 217 people answered an ad for "people who experience chills to music"; after five rounds of screening, eight remained. Each brought ten pieces of instrumental music that reliably gave them chills (about 3.7 chills per excerpt) and lay in a PET scanner while the team watched dopamine flow at their own favourite moments. The flip side is human too: some healthy people feel nothing from music yet react normally to money (#11).

**Best visual image**
A **15-second "waiting" window** sliding along our own waveform toward the 81.39 s drop. Inside the window one spot glows (尾状核 = "等待"); at the drop the glow jumps to a second spot (伏隔核 = "兑现"). Under it, Solberg & Dibben's **U-shaped loudness curve** (#30) traced over our track's real envelope: dip at the 49.1 s break, climb from 65.2 s, maximum at 81.39 s. Our track has their shape.

## Risks

- **Overclaiming the format (biggest risk).** "删掉那一刻，鸡皮疙瘩就没了" is not supported. The only causal study (#19) found *fewer* chills, marginal (p = 0.049), not zero. Use "明显变少" or "可能就没了，你自己试". No study shows that a breath before the drop gives more chills (#32), so make it a self-test. The 15 s is an analysis window, not a dopamine timer (#5).
- **Small samples** (n = 8, 20, 24, 38). Avoid "科学证明所有人…". Use "实验里 / 研究发现".
- **Drugs and sex (platform).** The Blood & Zatorre abstract says "food, sex, and drugs of abuse"; Ferreri uses levodopa/risperidone. On screen use only "美食" or "美食、金钱". Never name drugs or imply that a drug improves music. Do not use the press term "skin orgasm" (#22 title); say "颤栗 / frisson".
- **Medical framing.** Do not imply that people without chills are abnormal (#11, #12). "Musical anhedonia" is a research label, not a diagnosis for viewers. Avoid gender claims (sources conflict; Panksepp's female difference is UNVERIFIED).
- **Hearing safety and audio.** Sudden-loudness demos (#17, #18) must not clip or jump to painful levels on phones or earphones; the brief also bans pumping/clipping. Keep demo peaks no louder than the original mix.
- **Third-party music.** Do not play Sigur Rós, Holst, Mozart recordings or any song named in the studies; mention by name in text at most. Only the owner's bgm is audible.
- **Real people.** Researchers appear only as citations, with no faces (the brief bans realistic faces). No maps are involved.
- **Source hygiene.** Primaries for Blood & Zatorre (full text), Sloboda 1991, Grewe 2007 *MP*, Panksepp 1995 and Benedek 2011 were blocked. OK-2nd rows cite peer-reviewed summaries. If a platform asks, give the summarising paper's DOI.
