## Story-worthy

1. The sun god Ra was drawn with a falcon head in the day but a **ram's head** in his night barque (Louvre N 2699). Same god, two faces - good for the "他也是拉" beat.
2. Isis's crown is a **chair** (the throne sign) - she is "the throne" that makes a king (Met 96.4.4 shows it clearly as a green seat glyph).
3. The head-shapes of Sekhmet and Tefnut are identical on objects (lioness + disc + uraeus); only the inscription tells them apart (Louvre N 2699 vs N 1) - the film can use that: "同一张脸，两个女儿".
4. Osiris's green is the green of vegetation and rebirth, not sickness (UCL blog on Petrie UC14226; Met 96.4.4).
5. Nut's star-spangled body is **less common than the posters suggest**: stars appear in only about a quarter of 118 coffin-vignettes studied by Graur; the Met panel 96.4.4 paints her plain blue. Safe wording: "天空女神努特".

Strongest human story: Nauny's Book of the Dead (Met 30.3.31) - a chantress in c. 1050 BCE paid for exactly the scene ep60 told (Anubis at the scale, Thoth the baboon on top, Osiris waiting) - links episode 1 and this family.
Best visual image: the Met panel 96.4.4 - Isis (throne), Osiris (white atef, green skin, red wrappings), ibis-headed Thoth under the blue arch of Nut with a winged sun: five of the sixteen in one 31 cm board, on ochre yellow. It is almost the finished family-tree style frame.

## Risks

- **Unverified, do not use as "attested" on screen:** Geb's goose and green skin (I5); nu-pot on Nut's head (I6); Set's red/ochre colour, squared ears and forked tail (I9); Sekhmet's red dress (I14); painted colours for Shu, Tefnut, Atum, Horus (pschent), Ma'at; BM number EA 10554 (G2).
- Where several skin colours are attested (Ra: red-brown or yellow; Anubis: black head with red-brown body or green chest; Thoth: green or blue), the storyboard should pick one and not caption it as "the" colour.
- Identification caveat: for Met 96.4.4 the identifications of Nut (arch) and Thoth (ibis head) are mine from iconography; the Met text (blocked) may name them differently. Isis and Osiris match the Met API tags.
- Tefnut/Sekhmet and Set/Nephthys objects are labelled by Louvre cataloguers; some Louvre labels carry "(?)".
- Platform: no real people on screen; figures are deities, no gore planned. Set's story (the chest) is from Plutarch, ancient source; keep as "chest closes". No maps.
- If Douyin fact-check asks: all Met/Louvre rows are museum catalogue records; the Met accession numbers can be re-checked with the public API; BM rows need a browser check.
