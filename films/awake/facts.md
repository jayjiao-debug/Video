# 《越熬越精神》 facts (checked 2026-10-20)

| # | claim used on screen | source |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Sleepiness = sleep pressure (rises the longer you are awake) vs the circadian alerting signal (two-process model) | Borbély 1982 (textbook knowledge) |
| S2 | The circadian clock's alerting is strongest ~1–3 h before habitual bedtime: the "forbidden zone for sleep" / wake maintenance zone | Lavie 1986 (Electroencephalogr Clin Neurophysiol); Strogatz, Kronauer & Czeisler 1987 — via Wikipedia "Second wind (sleep)" |
| S3 | Chinese average bedtime 00:18 in 2025, 17 min later than the year before; ~80 % of college students sleep after midnight | Chinese Sleep Research Society, World Sleep Day 2025 release, wearable data of 150,000+ people (China Daily, 2025-03-24) |
| S4 | Chinese adults sleep 7.06–7.18 h on average; 48.5 % report sleep problems | 《2025年中国睡眠健康调查报告》 (China CDC survey, released by CSRS) |
| S5 | 4 h of light-emitting e-reader before bed → ~10 min longer to fall asleep, less REM, groggier next morning (n = 12) | Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy & Czeisler 2015, PNAS 112(4) |
| S6 | Bedtime procrastination: going to bed later than intended with no external reason, while knowing it's bad; named 2014 | Kroese et al. 2014, Frontiers in Psychology (via Scientific American) |
| S7 | "Revenge bedtime procrastination" (报复性熬夜) is a term that came from China | Psychology Today, 2021 |
| S8 | Short-video addiction correlates with poorer sleep quality, r = 0.456 (337 Henan college students; cross-sectional) | Frontiers in Psychology 2023, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1287735 |
| S9 | Variable reward (not knowing if the next one pays off) is the schedule that keeps behaviour going longest — the slot-machine schedule | Skinner (textbook knowledge) |
| S10 | Sleep inertia: worst in the first ~3 min after waking; cognition worse than after extended sleep deprivation; "may be as bad as or worse than being legally drunk"; worst effects fade within ~10 min, detectable up to 2 h | Wertz, Ronda, Czeisler & Wright 2006, JAMA 295:163 (CU Boulder release) |
| S11 | Waking from slow-wave (deep) sleep gives more inertia than from light/REM sleep | Tassi & Muzet 2000; Wikipedia "Sleep inertia" |
| S12 | Social jetlag: a body clock later than the alarm; linked with overweight | Roenneberg et al. 2012, Current Biology |
| S13 | Light in the morning advances the clock; a fixed wake time anchors it | standard circadian practice (AASM / sleep hygiene) |

Rules: no "blue light ruins your sleep" claim beyond S5's ~10 minutes. No diagnosis talk. The 00:18 → 21:18–23:18 window is
