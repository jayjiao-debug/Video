# 《身体里的两个人》 facts (checked 2026-10-20; Z3 updated and Z9–Z13 added for script v2)

| # | claim | source |
|---|---|---|
| Z1 | "Several people in one body" is dissociative identity disorder, a different condition; schizophrenia is not split personality | standard psychiatry (DSM-5 / ICD-11 are separate categories) |
| Z2 | Bleuler coined "schizophrenia" in Berlin on 24 April 1908; "splitting of psychic functioning" (schizo = split, phren = mind) | American Journal of Psychiatry 2008 (psychiatryonline, doi 10.1176/appi.ajp.2008.08050714) |
| Z3 | **1 in 300 people worldwide, about 27 million**, live with schizophrenia (the older WHO figure was ~24 million, 0.32 %) | WHO fact sheet "Schizophrenia", updated 11 Sep 2026, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/schizophrenia (accessed 2026-10-20) |
| Z4 | China: schizophrenia and other psychotic disorders, weighted lifetime prevalence 0.7 % (schizophrenia alone 0.6 %) | Huang et al., Lancet Psychiatry 2019 (China Mental Health Survey), via 搜狐转载 — not used in v2 |
| Z5 | Luhrmann (Stanford anthropologist): 60 people with schizophrenia, 20 each in San Mateo (US), Chennai, Accra. US voices harsh and threatening, none mainly positive; Chennai 11 of 20 heard family members, often elders advising; Accra 10 predominantly positive, 16 heard God. Her reading: cultural expectations shape how voices are heard (an interpretation, phrased "研究者认为") | Stanford News 2014-07-16; British Journal of Psychiatry 2015 |
| Z6 | Japan renamed 精神分裂病 → 統合失調症 ("integration disorder") in 2002 to reduce stigma; the old term almost vanished from newspapers after 2004, but the share of crime words in headlines did not change (~24.5 %) | Koike et al. 2015, via Asian Scientist — v2 uses only the 2002 rename |
| Z7 | Taiwan renamed 精神分裂症 → 思覺失調症 from 7 May 2014, to remove stigma | 卫生福利部 2014-06-24 |
| Z8 | 12356 national psychological assistance hotline, unified nationwide from 1 May 2025, round the clock | 新华网 2025-05-08 |
| Z9 | Eugen Bleuler (1857–1939), professor and director of the Burghölzli asylum in Zurich 1898–1927; proposed the new name to replace Kraepelin's "dementia praecox" (早发性痴呆), having found the illness was "not invariably incurable" and did not always progress to dementia | AJP 2008 (as Z2), https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2008.08050714 ; years: ASU Embryo Project, "Paul Eugen Bleuler (1857–1939)", https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/paul-eugen-bleuler-1857-1939 |
| Z10 | South Korea renamed 정신분열병 ("split-mind disorder") → **조현병 (調絃病)**, "attunement disorder"; 조현 = tuning the strings of a stringed instrument, a metaphor for tuning the mind and a signal that recovery is possible. Name confirmed by the Korean Medical Association in March 2011, in use from 2011–2012. About 70 % of practitioners surveyed said renaming alone cannot remove stigma | Cho et al., "Effects of Renaming Schizophrenia in Korea", Psychiatry Investigation 2018;15(7):656–662, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6056692 ; 약학정보원 (Korea Pharmaceutical Information Center) review "조현병의 모든 것 이해하기(1)", health.kr |
| Z11 | "A range of effective care options for people with schizophrenia exists, and at least one in three people with schizophrenia will be able to fully recover." | WHO fact sheet, as Z3 |
| Z12 | Onset is most often during late adolescence and the twenties (earlier in men than in women) | WHO fact sheet, as Z3 |
| Z13 | More than two out of three people with psychosis do not receive specialist mental health care (only 29 % do) | WHO fact sheet, as Z3 — not used in v2 (kept in reserve) |

Shared character (for the drop): 統合**失調**症 (Japan), **調**絃病 / 조현병 (Korea), 思覺**失調**症 (Taiwan): all three new names contain 調 (调); none keeps 分裂.

Rules: no claim that people with schizophrenia are dangerous (the evidence is nuanced: most are not violent; risk rises with
substance use and no treatment) — the film does not go there. No diagnosis talk, no figure acting out symptoms.
"Hearing voices is the most common hallucination" is textbook knowledge (DSM-5); phrase without numbers.
Onset age is now sourced to WHO (Z12).
