# PAPER STARS — Treatment

**Format:** animated short, ~2 minutes, no dialogue
**Genre:** magical-realist drama
**Audience:** all ages

## Logline

In a city so bright that no one has seen a star in years, a girl on a rooftop
folds her own out of paper, and slowly gets a whole city to turn off its
lights and look up.

## Theme

What we can't see isn't gone; we've just covered it up with our own light.
One small, patient act of wonder can spread from window to window until a
whole city chooses to see differently.

## Characters

**MIRA (10)** is small and quiet and stubborn about it. She wears a scarf that
is always a little too long, and she sits on the roof of her apartment
building every night. Her grandmother once told her the sky used to be full.
She has never seen it that way.

**THE CITY** hums and glows orange and never sleeps. Thousands of windows,
thousands of people, and nobody looks up.

**THE NEIGHBOR** lives on the next rooftop over. Mira has never met them.

## Story

**Prologue: No Stars.** At night the skyline blazes: office towers,
apartment blocks, the blue flicker of televisions. Above it the sky is a flat
brown-orange haze with nothing in it. A blinking light crosses it, but it's
only a plane.

**The Rooftop.** Mira sits on the ledge of her roof beside a rusted water tower,
feet dangling, scarf moving in the wind. She searches the sky. When a light
blinks overhead she points, then sees it's the plane, and lowers her hand.

**The Fold.** Mira takes a strip of paper and ties it into a knot, a small
pentagon. She pinches the edges and it puffs into a tiny five-pointed star,
the kind you fold by the hundred to fill a jar. It begins to glow.

**The Release.** She raises her cupped hands and lets it go. The paper star
drifts upward and stays there, the only star in the sky. Mira looks at the jar
beside her, which is full, and opens the lid. Dozens of paper stars pour out
and rise into the wind.

**The Drift.** The wind carries them out over the city, a slow river of warm
little lights. In the windows below, people notice. Silhouettes lean to the
glass and point up. Then one person does the obvious thing and switches their
light off to see better. Then someone else does.

**Lights Out.** It spreads from window to window and block to block, *click,
click-click, click*, a ripple of darkness moving outward across the whole
skyline. The orange haze drains out of the sky. One stubborn window stays lit
for a long moment, then it clicks off too.

**The Reveal.** Total darkness and silence, except for the paper stars. Then,
slowly, the real sky comes back: first a handful of stars, then hundreds, then
thousands, and then the Milky Way arcing over the rooftops. Mira's paper stars
drift upward and join them.

**Together.** Mira leans back on her hands, looking up. On the next rooftop
over, someone else is doing the same. The neighbor waves. Mira waves back. A
shooting star streaks across the sky, and both of them point at it together.

**Epilogue.** We tilt up into the sky, which is full.

*Look up.*
