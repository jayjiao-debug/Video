# Script: 上火，到底是什么火？  (v1, 2026-10-21)

**Logline:** 吃完火锅又熬夜，嘴里冒出溃疡，妈说"你上火了"。可"上火"至今没有明确定义：它背后是几种真实、各不相同的身体反应；"火"是古人给身体分类时用的比喻（希腊人也有一把火）；中医真正厉害的，是留下来的观察，而砒霜治白血病的故事，展示了观察怎样经过验证变成药。
**Takeaway:** 老祖宗留下的观察很珍贵；哪些该信，要靠验证。
**Length:** 175.2 s of lines + ~4 s end card = ~2:59 (under the 3-minute cap; set by the story). All times are provisional until the BGM is set: shift the whole block so the title gap (14.6–17.6) sits on the hardest drop.
**Tone:** starts as a small, familiar annoyance (sore mouth, mum's message) → curious (where is the fire?) → respectful wonder (two ancient worlds) → tense detective story (poison, a deadly disease) → warm relief (能治好的) → back to the mirror, smiling.

## Title options
1. **《上火，到底是什么火？》** (8 字) — the question every viewer has half-asked themselves; the word 上火 is the search term and the share hook; it promises an answer without giving one, and it fits what the film shows (we look for the "fire" and find several real things plus an old metaphor). Recommended.
2. **《妈说你上火了》** (6 字) — pure viewer situation and the send-to prompt in one: the viewer recognises the sentence at once and thinks of who says it. Doesn't hint at the answer at all. Risk: reads like a sketch, so the cover image has to carry the science/history promise.
3. **《从上火到砒霜》** (6 字) — the biggest curiosity gap: how do you get from a mouth ulcer to a poison? It names where the film ends up but not what happens there (it doesn't say arsenic cures anything, so it can't overclaim). Risk: a health-topic fact-check may look harder at "砒霜" on the cover; never pair it with a cure word on the cover.
