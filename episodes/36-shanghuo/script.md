**Logline:** 吃完火锅又熬夜，嘴里冒出溃疡，妈说"你上火了"，可连中医研究者都说它没有明确定义。症状是真的，背后是几种各不相同的原因；"火"是古人给世界分类时的比喻，希腊人也有一把火。真正珍贵的是记下来的观察，砒霜的故事展示了观察怎样经过验证，变成一种药。
**Takeaway:** 老祖宗留下的观察很珍贵；哪些该信，验证过才知道。
**Length:** lines 0–159.9 s (holds included) + ~4 s end card ≈ 2:44, leaving about 6 s for beat snapping under 2:50. All times are provisional until the BGM is set. Shift the whole block so the title gap (11.7–14.7) lands on the hardest drop.
**Tone:** a small, familiar annoyance → curious → respectful wonder → a tense detective story → warm relief → back at the mirror, smiling at mum's message.

## Title options
1. **《上火，到底是什么火？》** (8 字). The question every viewer has half-asked; 上火 is the search word. It promises an answer without giving one, and it fits exactly what the film shows. **Director's pick and mine.** (Cold reader: clear, but could read as a standard 科普 account, so the cover image has to do the surprising work: e.g. the mirror ulcer with the 火 stamp and the blank 定义：＿＿ card.)
2. **《妈说你上火了》** (6 字). The viewer's own situation and the send-to prompt in one. Risk (cold read): it can set up "prove mum wrong" or a comedy skit. The film is kinder than that, so this title undersells it.
3. **《从上火到砒霜》** (6 字). The biggest curiosity gap; the cold reader would click it first. **Risk:** a careless reader, or parents' generation, could read it as "砒霜治上火" or "上火的东西像毒药". Neither is in the film, and the first is dangerous for a health topic. Use it only with a cover that makes the leukaemia/验证 direction clear, never with a cure word, and expect closer fact-check attention.
