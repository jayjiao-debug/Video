## Story-worthy

**The most surprising true facts**
1. **"火曰炎上"**: the word 上火 goes back to the oldest wuxing text. 《尚书·洪范》 defines fire as that which "burns and ascends" (C2). The metaphor is about 2,500 years old, and it was a *description* of fire, not a claim about cells.
2. **Your hot-pot "fire" won a Nobel Prize.** Chilli doesn't taste hot. Capsaicin switches on TRPV1, the same nerve sensor that fires at more than about 43°C. The brain gets a "burning" signal (B9–B11, Nobel 2021). The ancients felt something real and named it 火.
3. **One night up until 3 a.m. → the next morning, inflammation genes in immune cells are switched up more than 3-fold** (IL-6 mRNA, Irwin 2006, B4). The "上火 after a late night" feeling has a measurable cousin, but its name is inflammation, not fire.
4. **Arsenic, the classic poison, became a standard cure.** A rural healer's mix of arsenic, mercury and toad venom was tested and stripped down to the one chemical that worked. Today ATRA + arsenic, with **no chemotherapy**, is Europe's standard for non-high-risk APL. In the final trial 99.2% were alive at about 4 years, against 92.6% on chemotherapy, and relapse was 1.9% vs 13.9% (D18, D19).
5. **The discoverer was nearly forgotten.** Zhang Tingdong's 1970s papers "have not been cited by a single English paper" (as of 2013, D10). He was honoured only in 2020, at 88 (born 1932), with the Future Science Prize (D24).
6. (bonus) Both China and Greece built a whole-body "fire" system at about the same time: 五行 (Warring States) and the four humours (5th c. BCE). In Greece, yellow bile was "hot and dry … fire, youth and summer" (C10). The Greek system was dropped in the 1800s. The observations survived, and the explanations did not.

**The strongest human story (arsenic)**
- The detective turn: Zhang Tingdong's team gets a vague folk recipe that "cures cancer". Instead of using it on everything, from 1972 they focus on leukaemia. Going back through their own records (1973–78, 55 patients), they notice it works best on one rare type, the 早幼粒 (promyelocytic) kind, and say so twice in a provincial journal in 1979 (D5, D7). Then they remove the ingredients that don't work, finally including the mercury in 1996 (D9). That is the scientific method done in a Harbin hospital during the Cultural Revolution, published in Chinese, and ignored abroad for decades (D10).
- The payoff number: of 32 APL patients treated 1974–85, **16 were still alive more than five years later**, for a disease that used to kill in "only weeks" (D8, D1).
- Parallel human moment (ATRA side): in 1986 a 5-year-old girl in Shanghai became the first APL patient in the world cured by oral ATRA (D12). No name and no face. Show a silhouette or an empty hospital bed with a toy at most.

**Best visual image**
- **The recipe being filtered.** Three jars (two powdered "rocks" plus toad venom, D4) pour into a test tube. One by one the useless and toxic ones are crossed out until a single white powder, As₂O₃, remains, and that one becomes a modern IV bag. It shows "observation → test → keep only what works" in one continuous move.
- Alternative: the **"fire" symbol splitting** into real, labelled things. The 火 character over a mouth breaks into: bitten cheek / burn from hot food / stress / no sleep → IL-6 ↑ / virus / TRPV1 at 43°C.
- Alternative: **two ancient wheels side by side**, the Chinese 五行 cycle and the Greek four-humour square, each with its own "fire" slot glowing.

## Risks

- **Health-claim wording to avoid:** no "吃X降火/去火", no herbal or drug advice. A5 is a herbal-decoction trial, so don't show its treatment. Never say "上火不存在" (the symptoms are real) or "上火被科学证实了" (A3/A4 show it is not defined). Say: "症状是真的，'火'是古人的比喻". Associations (B3, B5, B8) must read "更容易 / 有关", never "导致".
- **Cure wording:** sources range from "about 80%" to "85–90% long-term survival" to 99% in trials (D19–D21). Only use "大多数APL患者可以被治愈" / "高度可治愈". Trial numbers go on screen only with their condition ("试验中，4年多后…"). Population data still show about 17% early deaths (D21). Never "砒霜能治白血病/癌症" in general. It works on **one subtype (APL)**, together with ATRA, as a pharmaceutical-grade IV drug under medical supervision. Add a frame or caption: "医用药品，切勿自行服用". Arsenic is a poison, and Douyin will flag anything that reads like a home remedy.
- **Contested history:** who first learned the folk remedy, and when (Han Taiyun 1971 vs Zhang's team visiting in 1972, D3/D4). Use "1970年代初，哈尔滨的医生们". Credit is also contested between Harbin and Shanghai, which is a known, sensitive debate. Give both their due (Harbin found it, Shanghai proved and explained it and built the combination, Europe confirmed it in randomized trials) and don't rank them. The practitioner's name and village (林甸) are **UNVERIFIED**. The 1991 vs 1992 date for the 32-case report is unclear, so leave the year out.
- **Real people:** 张亭栋, 王振义, 陈竺, 陈赛娟, 屠呦呦 are real and living or recently living public figures. Use names and facts only, no likenesses, or a neutral text card. Patients: no names, faces, photos or ages beyond the sourced "5岁小女孩" (D12). Respectful tone for any mention of deaths.
- **Cultural Revolution:** mention only as a time marker ("文革期间", as in D10). No political commentary.
- **TCM respect:** brief forbids mockery. Frame 五行 and the four humours as "古人整理世界的方式", with the same respect for both. The good-faith lines are A6 (HealthHub's "2,000 years of detailed observation") and D25.
- **Unverified (screenwriter may not use):** a 上火 prevalence %, 火↔心 correspondence (C7), the exact FDA date, the practitioner's name/village, "no real temperature change in the mouth".
- **Platform:** health topic, so put the sources list on the end card. Use the 自主声明 "内容由AI生成". No maps.
