## Corrections to the brief (read first)

1. **「2400年前」 should read 「2400多年前」.** 新华社 (2025-04-18) says the 曾侯乙 chime-bell inscriptions keep 「2400多年前人类的音乐记忆」 and that the tomb is 「战国早期」; 新华社 (2025-10-31): the tomb's bamboo slips are 「距今约2400年，年代在战国早期」. 湖北省博物馆's own page says only 「曾侯乙生活在公元前五世纪」. The exact year 「前433年」 appears only in a 澎湃 self-media (历史心发现) post, so it is **not** usable. 2026 − 433 = 2459 years (估算, only if that year is right); 「2400多年前」 holds either way.
2. **「冰箱」 must be attributed.** 湖北省博物馆文物保护中心主任江旭东 (中新社, 2025-07-22) puts 「冰箱」 in quotation marks: 「中国古代的"冰箱"铜鉴缶」. 李零 (北京大学教授, 澎湃 2017-07-26) says 「冰鉴，就是古代的冰箱」 but adds 「冰鉴放冰块，属于冷冻」 and that mechanical 「制冷」 fridges were invented in the 19th century. So the honest hook is that this "fridge" **has no cold of its own: the cold is winter ice** (rows B3, C1-C4). Do not write 「世界上最早的冰箱」 as fact: 扬子晚报 only says it is "possibly" that (⚠, journalist's hedge), and 世界日报 quotes the museum director 许杰 calling it 「世界最古老的冰箱」 at an exhibition in San Francisco (⚠).
3. **The object's name.** 曾侯乙墓's report calls it 「鉴缶」 (李零: 「称鉴称缶，都只说对一半，曾侯乙墓的报告叫"鉴缶"，才比较合适」). 国家博物馆's exhibition page calls the Hubei piece 「铜方鉴缶」 and says texts call such vessels 「冰盘」 and 「冰鉴」. 「铜冰鉴」 is the popular name. On screen: **「曾侯乙墓的铜鉴缶（冰鉴）」**.
4. **《周礼》's 鉴 is not the 曾侯乙 object.** 《周礼》 has 「祭祀，共冰鉴」; 郑玄注 (as quoted in 《太平御览》) says 「鉴如瓶，大口，藏冰以御温气也」 (a wide-mouthed vessel). 曾侯乙's piece is a **square bronze basin holding a jar**. Don't say the 《周礼》 vessel "is" the 曾侯乙 one.
5. **The two in the pair.** 李零: a pair came from the tomb, one now in the 国家博物馆, one in the 湖北省博物馆. 湖北省博物馆's piece is the one 国博's 「凤凰故国」 page shows. Two other sources give different sizes (B5). Don't put a size on screen.
6. **冰酪 is not Tang.** Press ties 冰酪 to the Yuan (中新网 2022; 仲富兰 via Marco Polo, ⚠). The Tang dessert in the sources is **酥山**. Don't write 「唐代冰酪」.
7. **硝石制冰 = no primary source found.** See section F. Every mention I opened is un-sourced press (中新网, 新民周刊, 新民晚报, 搜狐). Marked ✗ for on-screen use.
8. **凌人 headcount is 94 (估算 from the 《周礼》 list), not 80.** 澎湃 (赵尧) says 「一般有80人」; the 《周礼》 text lists 徒八十人 plus 14 others (D1).
9. **冰票 is real** (《燕京岁时记》, Qing) and **「冰盏」 is real but belonged to 酸梅汤 sellers**, not ice sellers (G3, G4). The brief's 「卖冰的冰盏」 is not quite right.
10. **ctext.org** returned a bot-check; the 《周礼》 text comes from 南京博物院's site and 《太平御览》 (原古诗文网). Spot-check on ctext before publishing.
11. **Not opened / blocked:** 国家统计局 yearbook 2025 tables 6-10 / 6-15 (urban / rural fridges per 100 households, 2024) exist only as .jpg and were blocked, so **the urban/rural 2024 split is UNVERIFIED**. 湖北省博物馆 pages (hbww.org.cn), 国博 article 《青铜冰鉴：穿越千年的清凉》, 光明网 2025-07-18, 广州政协 (万宝) page: all robots-blocked. people.com.cn sub-pages: redirect loop.

## Story-worthy

**The 5 most surprising facts (all ✓ unless marked):**
1. **The "fridge" had no cold of its own.** The 曾侯乙 鉴缶 needed winter ice packed between basin and jar (B3, B7, 李零: 「属于冷冻」). The cold of 2400多年前 summers was cut in December.
2. **《周礼》: store three times as much ice as you need.** 「令斩冰，三其凌」; 清代 cellars lost about 2/3 over the summer (C3, C4). The ice office had 94 staff (C5, 估算).
3. **The same vessel could be warm in winter.** 江旭东: 「鉴缶具有温热与冰镇双重功能」 (B4).
4. **A Song street in June: blue umbrellas, and the best ice stalls used silver vessels.** 《东京梦华录》 「冰雪惟旧宋门外两家最盛，悉用银器」 (E6).
5. **Qing ice came with a voucher.** 工部 issued 冰票; officials queued for ice by rank (D3). And a sour-plum-drink vendor clanged two brass bowls: 「敲冰盏」 (G3).

Runners-up: 凌阴 at 秦雍城: 190 m³ (C6); Tang nobles had ice carved into mountains at a banquet (E2); 酥山 (E3); 2024: 104.8 fridges per 100 households vs rural 1.22 in 1990 (A1, A3); China built 1.09亿 fridges in 2025 (A2); the 乾隆 "冰箱" cooled a room through coin-shaped holes (G6).

**Strongest human story:** a minister in Chu who dodged an unwanted job by pretending to be ill and sleeping on a bed above ice in a fur coat (C8, ⚠ from 李零 only; check the 《左传》 襄公二十一年 text before use), or, fully ✓, the Qing office clerk holding a 冰票 stamped by the 工部 and walking to a cellar in summer (D3). A softer ✓ human image: the 酸梅汤 seller clinking two bowls down the lane (G3).

**Best visual images:**
- The hook: a viewer's phone with an ice drink; a fridge door opens and inside is a square bronze vessel with a block of ice in the gap (B3).
- A winter night, chisels ringing on a frozen river (G1, C1), then a sloped pit being filled (C6).
- One hand holding the cold thing as the walker moves: a chunk of ice (周) → the bronze 鉴缶 (战国) → mountain-shaped ice (唐) → a bowl under a blue umbrella (宋) → two brass bowls clinking (清) → a generic fridge (现代).
- A rack of 冰票 slips beside a cellar door (D3).
