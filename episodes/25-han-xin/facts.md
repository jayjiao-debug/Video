## Story-worthy

**5 most surprising facts**
1. **The original problem has no soldiers and no 韩信.** The 4th–5th-century text just says 「今有物不知其數」. Yang Hui (1275) called it 「秦王暗點兵」 and Song folk names were 「鬼谷算」「隔牆算」. 韩信's name is documented only by Ming 1592 (程大位). The general was attached to the puzzle later (rows 1, 16–20).
2. **Sunzi's own working gives 233, not 23.** He adds 140+63+30 = 233 and then subtracts 210. The "wrong-looking" big number is itself a correct answer, which sets up the catch (row 4, row 32).
3. **The method from 1,600 years ago sits inside today's RSA keys.** RFC 8017 stores "CRT exponents" dP, dQ and a "CRT coefficient" qInv in every RSA private key in this form (rows 28–30).
4. **Qin Jiushao (1247) solved 377873x ≡ 1 (mod 499067) by hand** for a calendar, answer 457999. I checked it, and it is correct (row 13).
5. **Gauss used the same trick for calendars.** Three cycles of 15, 19 and 28 years pin down a year within a 7980-year period, with multipliers 6916, 4200 and 4845. This is the 70/21/15 idea at scale (row 23).

**Strongest human story:** 秦九韶. He was a brilliant mathematician whose book records a universal method, yet contemporaries called him violent and corrupt, and he was dismissed in 1258 for bribery (DSB, row 15). Alternative: the anonymous "孙子", of whom nothing is known beyond one book (row 8).

**Best visual image:** three regiments of the same crowd forming up in rows of 3, then 5, then 7, with a few people left over each time (2, 3, 2). Three tiny numbers on a scroll then light up as 「二十三」. Then 70 / 21 / 15 dial around a 105-step ring, landing on 23, 128, 233… as the ring keeps turning. For the modern callback, a padlock (RSA key) splits into p and q, the two halves compute, and qInv stitches them back together.

## Risks
- **Legend vs history:** do not show 韩信 actually doing this as fact. Wording: 「韩信点兵是后来的传说」. The 《史记》 claim (row 21) is UNVERIFIED, so do not cite 《史记》.
- **Book names:** 周密's source is reported as 《志雅堂杂钞》, not 《癸辛杂识》 as the brief suggested, and both are unconfirmed in a primary text. Say 「宋代民间叫它“鬼谷算”」 without naming the book.
- **Dates with disagreement:** 《孙子算经》's date (3rd–5th c.): use 「1600多年前」. Matthiessen 1874 vs 1881: omit the year.
- **Real people:** 韩信 (historical general) appears only as the legend's name. Qin Jiushao's misconduct comes from historical accusations, so attribute it (「据记载」) if used.
- **Maps:** none needed. If showing 汉/宋 China, avoid national borders (brief rule).
- **Platform risk:** low. There is no violence; soldiers in formation should be neutral and stylised (no weapons pointed or battle). The RSA/crypto content is standard, public IETF material with no security-misuse angle. Do not invent a speed-up figure for RSA-CRT.
- **Source caveats for platform replies:** primary text via Project Gutenberg #24038 plus 《數學傳播》 (Academia Sinica), because ctext.org refused automated access. Rows 16–19 and 25 rest on university lecture notes or 《数学文化》 (secondary). Everything else rests on Shen 1988 (DOI 10.1007/BF00357063), RFC 8017, the DSB, and Academia Sinica journal articles.
