# Facts table: 你以为大家都在看你

Every claim on screen appears here with a credible source (official report, government/UN body, peer-reviewed
paper with DOI, national archive/museum, established news agency). Never Wikipedia, 百度百科, 知乎, blogs.

Researcher: 2026-10-07. **All studies below are small lab studies, mostly with US college students (Cornell,
Williams, Northwestern, Yale)**; the liking-gap paper also has community/workshop samples. Say "实验里" / "研究发现",
never "所有人都…".

How the papers were read: the published article text, opened as full-text copies (no paywall bypass). Cite the
DOI in the end card. Copies used: 2000 spotlight paper = scanned copy of the JPSP article
(https://www.psychologyib.com/uploads/1/1/7/5/11758934/the_spotlight_effect_-_ib_psychology.pdf); 2001 paper =
PDF on co-author Nicholas Epley's own site; 2018 liking gap = PDF on the Wharton faculty site; 2021 children
paper = PDF on the Tomasello lab site (Duke).

## Story-worthy

**5 most surprising numbers**
1. **46% vs 23%**: students in the embarrassing shirt guessed about half the room noticed; about a quarter actually did (exactly 2×). (#7)
2. **48% vs 8%**: with a shirt they were *proud* of, they still guessed about half; under one in ten noticed (6×). Good news and bad news in one: your blunders and your best moments both get less attention than you think. (#9)
3. **Got 1 of 10 quiz questions right**, expected to be rated about 34/100 (well below average); the people watching rated them close to average. (#16; use the safe wording)
4. **Embarrassing secret in your intro**: expected a 52/100 impression, actually got 69. (#18)
5. **Liking gap at 5 years old**: kids from age 5 already think their playmate likes them less than they like the playmate, and among Yale first-years the gap lasted from September until May. (#20, #21)

**Best cold-open line** (viewer's own moment, frame 0):
"你穿了件尴尬的T恤走进教室，觉得一半的人都看见了。" → turn: "实际上，只有四分之一。" (from #7; holds exactly: 46% → 23%).
Alternative for the 2nd drop: "就连你最得意的那件衣服——注意到的人，不到十分之一。" (#9)

**Strongest human story:** the Study 1 setup itself (#3–#7). A student pulls on the shirt, walks into a room of 4–6 peers who all face the door, and is told "on second thought, wait outside" just as he's about to sit. Then: "How many of them could tell who's on your shirt?" He's sure it's half; it's a quarter. One person, one door, one turn.

**Best visual image:** the percentage meter. A big gauge labelled "你以为" fills to 46%, then a second needle labelled "实际" drops to 23%. Reuse it at 48% → 8% for the "good shirt". For the 3D set-piece, the real room was small: one table, 4–6 seated people facing a doorway, one person standing in it. The pull-back reveal (everyone in their own little light) fits that true layout better than a lecture hall.

## Risks

- **Celebrity names:** Barry Manilow (living singer) is in the paper as the "embarrassing" shirt. Name only, no face, no drawing, no likeness on the shirt (blank shirt or abstract pattern + text "某位歌手"). Frame it as "a shirt *students in 1990s Cornell* found embarrassing to wear", never "Manilow is embarrassing". Study 2's "proud" shirts used Bob Marley, Jerry Seinfeld and Martin Luther King Jr.; leave those names out (MLK as "a T-shirt to be proud of" is easy to misread). Vanilla Ice (Study 4): skip.
- **Overclaiming:** the brief already bans "没人在看你". All numbers come from small samples (Study 1: 15 targets; Study 2: 15 targets). Say "一个实验里" and "比你以为的少得多". Do not generalise to "everyone/always".
- **Population:** US college students (Cornell, Williams, Northwestern, Yale) in lab settings, 1990s–2010s. Not Chinese samples. One honest line in the description or end card is enough ("美国大学生实验").
- **Replication status:** I found **no large independent replication** (Many Labs / Registered Replication Report) of the Manilow T-shirt study. The spotlight-effect papers are mostly by the same author group (Gilovich, Medvec, Savitsky, Epley, Kruger). The liking gap **has** an independent conceptual replication in children (Wolf, Nafe & Tomasello 2021, N = 241; #21). If a platform reviewer asks, say: "原始实验，样本小，后续多为同一研究团队的扩展研究".
- **Unverified items:** the illusion-of-transparency numbers (1998) and the speech-anxiety paper (2003) are UNVERIFIED (#22, #23). There is no source for "how fast people forget" (#24). Screenwriter must not use their numbers.
- **Sensitive details:** the bedwetting detail (#18). Say "尴尬的小秘密". Social anxiety: this is not therapy advice. Avoid "治好社恐"; "少一点内耗" is fine.
- **Maps / real people on screen:** no maps. The only real people are the authors (named in the end card) and the celebrities above (name only).
- **Platform:** a psychology claim with DOI-backed numbers is low risk. Keep the DOIs in the end card / posting copy: 10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211, 10.1037/0022-3514.81.1.44, 10.1177/0956797618783714, 10.1177/0956797620980754.
