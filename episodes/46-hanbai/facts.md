## Story-worthy

**5 most surprising facts**
1. **The oldest record already says "probably".** Wu Di's own annals (汉书·武帝纪) list all eight of his Taishan trips and never mention a tree. About 600 years later 《水经注》 says the cypresses 「盖汉武所植也」, "probably planted by Wu Di" (rows 9, 12, 14).
2. **Rebels once hacked one, and the book says it bled.** 「赤眉嘗斫一樹，見血而止，今斧創猶存」. A tree in the temple is still called 「赤眉斧痕」 (row 10).
3. **Half of the famous tree is dead.** The west trunk burned. The east trunk lives on one strip of bark 32 cm wide (row 5).
4. **An emperor painted it, and then the painting faced the tree.** Qianlong painted it in 1762; the 2.9 m scroll is in the Palace Museum. In 1771 he had it carved on a stele beside the tree and asked: 「殿旁亭里相望近，名实主宾谁是乎？」, meaning "the real one by the hall and the painted one in the pavilion, which is the host and which the guest?" (rows 27–29).
5. **In 2024 its children went to guard the emperor who supposedly planted it.** Eight seedlings, six from 汉柏连理, went to 茂陵, Wu Di's tomb (row 43).

**Strongest human story:** the circle closes. An emperor came, maybe planted you, and left. Others came: 光武, 高宗, 玄宗, 真宗, then Qianlong ten times, and they left too. Rebels hacked you. Fire took half of you. Soldiers' cooking smoke killed dozens of your neighbours (1928–29), and the 唐槐 across the temple died. What kept you alive was a strip of bark, and today staff who number every tree and collect your seeds. In 2024 your seedlings were carried back to Wu Di's tomb to guard *him*. This fits the brief's second takeaway (「是谁让你活下来」) and gives the ending a twist: 「两千年后，轮到你的孩子去守他了」.

**Best visual image:** one tree with two trunks, one green and one charred black, in a courtyard of stone tablets. Behind it the temple changes (the main hall appears after 1008, a market in 1928, phones today), and beside it Qianlong's carved painting of the same tree looks back at it.

## Risks

- **Legend vs fact.** "Wu Di planted it" must be 相传 / 「据记载」 every time. The oldest source hedges (「盖」), and Wu Di's annals are silent (row 12). Do not write 「汉武帝亲手种下你」 without the label.
- **Count 5 vs 6.** Official sources differ (row 3). Avoid the number, or cite the 2025 register (6).
- **The age is not measured.** 「2135岁」 is our 估算 from a legend; 「2100多年」 is the official wording (rows 1, 13).
- **Processions passing the tree are UNVERIFIED** (row 24). Show emperors at 泰山, not "walking past you".
- **Kangxi** is verified only for a 1689 visit to the mountain (row 25); his visit to the temple is unverified.
- **Real people.** 孙良诚 and the 1928 provincial government (rows 30–31): keep them neutral and unnamed on screen. The Japanese bombing (row 34) is sensitive but documented; one beat, no gore. Avoid the 1967 Cultural-Revolution stele swap (lead only, UNVERIFIED, and politically sensitive). Living staff (马生平, 李秀芬, 姚起, 宋洪兵) appear only as quoted, with no likeness.
- **Images.** Qianlong's painting belongs to the Palace Museum. Draw our own tree; do not trace the painting or photos.
- **Maps.** None needed; avoid borders.
- **Platform.** 「最后一位封禅皇帝」 is sourced (row 21). 「第一个双遗产」 is sourced (row 38).
- **Blocked sources.** 史记·封禅书 and 汉书·郊祀志 could not be opened, so do not claim on screen that 「史记没写」.
