# Brief: 泰山汉柏 (ep46, working title)

- **Owner ask (2026-10-24 21:48):** 「有木有 2000 多年的树，树木一直在，然后背景还有人物之类的」.
- **Owner pick (21:50):** 泰山岱庙「汉柏」：皇帝来了又走，你一直在.
- **Episode:** ep46 · **Series:** VIBE知识大赏 (Juno), history-video-style format (iwanau 《假如你是传国玉玺》 rhythm, our own look).

## Idea

- **In one sentence:** the ~2,100-year life of one cypress at the Dai Temple (岱庙) at the foot of Mount Tai, told in the second person.
  - Tradition says Emperor Wu of Han planted it when he came to perform the 封禅 rites.
  - Emperors climb past it to talk to heaven, then leave; dynasties rise and fall in the background.
  - Fires, wars and storms hit the temple, and the tree is still standing in its courtyard today.
- **The device (new for the series):** the camera stays at the tree. 「你」 never moves. The world moves behind you: eras, people, buildings, weather and seasons.
  - ep41 walked a relay of people; ep43 and ep45 passed an object from hand to hand. Here the object stays and history comes to it.
- **Why an 18–25 viewer cares:**
  - Everyone knows 泰山 and 封禅 from textbooks and dramas.
  - Almost nobody knows a living tree there may have watched Han Wu Di, Tang Xuanzong, Song Zhenzong and Qianlong walk past.
  - 「你」 being the tree turns 2,000 years into one life, with a running counter.
- **Takeaway:** to be found by the screenwriter.
  - Direction: the emperors came to prove they were chosen by heaven and left; the one that stayed was a tree nobody had to prove anything about.
  - Or: who kept you alive was not the emperors but the people who watered and propped you up.
  - Find what the sources actually carry.
- **Counter:** 「你 X 岁」 from the traditional planting year to 2026.
  - About 2,136 if 110 BC (元封元年) holds; the researcher fixes the year.
  - A second counter is possible, e.g. 「第 N 位来看你的皇帝」.
- **Repeated set-piece (varies, speeds up):** an emperor's procession arrives at the tree, passes it on the way up the mountain, and leaves.
  - Each time it's shorter, a different dynasty's costume and flags, and something about the tree has changed (taller, a scar, a prop, a railing).
  - It ends with no emperor at all: tourists with phones.

## Format

- **Length target:** ≈2:30, max 3:00. 1920×1080, subtitle-first, music only, no voice unless the owner asks.
- **Music:** bgm2.mp3, cut with the episodes/42-yiyi/music/ scripts; the mp3 is never committed. The title lands on the hardest drop.
- **Hook:** the cold open starts with a 「假如说…」 line (owner note, ep45 21:39), e.g. 「假如说你是一棵树，站在同一个地方两千年」.
- **Rhythm (from the reference and the ep44 brief):**
  - The cold open lists fates fast (~1 event / 1.5 s) and ends on a present-day hook; the title comes after, at ~12 s.
  - One 「你」 plus the running counter.
  - Every beat is an event with a result or a twist, not quote after quote.
  - The ending question starts arguments in the comments.
  - Text is big top-left; lines stack and stay; punch lines are centred and bigger.

## Visual direction

- **Look:** episode-specific; see docs/history-video-style/.
  - Flat vector backgrounds; no generated painterly plates, no AI images.
  - 「你」 (the tree) is the only glowing colour.
  - Not the reference's red chip / red key words / serif seal furniture.
  - Not a straight copy of ep41–45's dusk-silhouette family. People may stay one-tone silhouettes, but the world around the tree needs its own palette (e.g. the stone, the mountain, the seasons).
- **The tree must be drawn from reference:** the real 汉柏 has a distinctive twisted trunk, and it's propped and fenced today. The researcher collects licence-clean reference descriptions; we draw our own.
- **Possible set-pieces:**
  - the same frame through four seasons in one move;
  - lightning or fire in the temple at night;
  - the 封禅 procession torches climbing the mountain behind the tree;
  - Qianlong painting the tree (if verified);
  - today's visitors' phone flashes.

## Must avoid

- Presenting legends as fact: 「汉武帝亲手种」 is tradition; label it 相传 or show the source.
- Inventing emperors' visits to *this tree*: only those the sources tie to 岱庙 or 泰山 封禅, labelled honestly.
- Maps with borders.
- Hands reaching in from off-screen.
- Copying the reference's look.
- Chest-beating the sources don't carry.
