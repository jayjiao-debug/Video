## Story-worthy

**5 most surprising facts**
1. 1/4 second is enough: listeners sorted 10 genres far above chance from 250 ms clips, and non-musicians (44%) did as
   well as music-theory students (43%) (row 1–2). Even 125 ms → ~54% with chance at 20% (row 5).
2. Dance music and your feet run at the same speed: people walk at ~2 steps per second (120/min), and a 74,000-song
   DJ list peaks at ~120 BPM (rows 7–8). House sits right there: 120–140 (row 9).
3. Slow a 130-BPM house track to 108 BPM and the room hears Latin music instead: that is how moombahton started at a
   2009 basement party (row 41). The film's whole thesis in one true story.
4. Swing isn't a fixed "2:1": real jazz drummers go from ~1:1 when fast to ~3.5:1 when slow (row 22).
5. Metal guitarists play only two notes on purpose: with heavy distortion a full chord sounds "dirty", a bare fifth
   stays clean and heavy (row 32). And "house" is named after one club, the Warehouse (row 40).

**Strongest human story:** the moombahton party (row 41): a DJ booked to play fast electro, a basement full of
teenagers who only wanted reggaeton, and one decision to drag a track from ~130 down to 108 BPM, which became a new
genre. It is relatable (a party, a playlist that isn't working) and it demonstrates the tempo clue live. Runner-up:
the Warehouse "We play house music" tavern sign (row 40).

**Best visual image:** one 16-step beat grid that never leaves the screen. The same 4×16 lights re-pattern for
each genre: the kick column lighting every beat (house), beat 1 going dark while beat 3 flashes (reggae one-drop:
"第一拍空着"), the hi-hat row turning into a blurred "zipper" (trap), the second dot of every pair visibly
sliding late (swing), and a tempo needle sliding 130 → 108 as house turns Latin. A footstep track along the
bottom at 2 steps/s is the ruler for "faster/slower than walking".

## Risks

- **Copyrighted recordings and real artists:** never play or imitate recognisably real songs (Heart of Glass,
  I Will Always Love You, Moombah, Metallica, Daft Punk, Bad Bunny). Everything is synthesized from our own melody
  (brief). Mention real songs by name only if needed; better to describe ("同一首歌，乡村→流行").
- **Ongoing litigation (row 43):** the Steely & Clevie dembow case is before a US jury → do not show it; don't say
  any artist "copied" anything. Dembow origin can be stated neutrally only if needed (row 43 first sentence).
- **Drug / violence associations (platform risk):** "trap" = drug-house slang (TIDAL article); reggae ↔ cannabis
  imagery; metal ↔ skulls/occult. Don't explain the word "trap", no leaf/smoke imagery, no skulls/pentagrams.
- **Brand/trademark:** draw a generic drum machine, not a Roland TR-808/909 with logo; no Lofi Girl character; no
  MPC/Ableton UI copies.
- **Flags/maps:** reggae's Jamaican or Rastafari colours as flags, maps of Kingston/Chicago/Rio → avoid maps with
  borders (owner rule); use colour accents without flags.
- **Cultural respect:** don't reduce genres to ethnic caricatures (dreadlocks, sombreros, etc.); show sound and
  instruments, not people's appearance.
- **Counting ambiguity:** reggae one-drop and trap snare are "beat 3" in one counting and "2 and 4"/"beat 2" in
  another (rows 24, 28). Screen wording must be the counting-independent version ("第一拍空着", "军鼓每小节只打一下").
- **Overclaim risk:** "1/4秒就能听出" must stay "above chance / 比瞎猜准得多", not "everyone can"; "dance music is 120
  because we walk at 120" is a comparison, not a proven cause (row 8).
