- **Idea in one sentence:** two boys born the same day, 12 Feb 1809, one in a Kentucky log cabin with no glass in the windows, one in a doctor's house in Shrewsbury. They never meet, yet their lives rhyme (mothers lost at 8 and 9, a young man who sees slavery and can't forget it, a child lost at 10 and 11). In 1859–1863 the two lines touch exactly once: Darwin, in a letter, cheering "your President's" fiat against slavery.
- **Suspense:** the names are withheld until the lines meet, at the drop. Until then it is 「左边的他」 and 「右边的他」, and the viewer guesses.
- **Why a 18–25 viewer cares:** both names are textbook names, but nobody knows they were born on the same day, or how similar their lives were. The ending question starts arguments: 「他们谁改变世界更多？」
- **Takeaway (direction):** two people who never met fought the same fight from two ends. One proved all life is one family tree; the other made 「人人生而平等」 law.
