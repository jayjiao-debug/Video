## Story-worthy: 5 most surprising facts (for 2:30)
1. **His mum's first words after 28 years: 「你去哪儿了？」** He hadn't seen her in ten years; she wept under a "Welcome Home Son" banner (rows 47, 48, 46, 51). (Rows 52–53 add that she saw him 3 times in 27 years and bought him a Christmas present every year, but those are single-source BBC: ?.)
2. **Rule: no wheels, so where the land ends, he swims.** He calls himself "definitely not a swimmer", yet swam nearly 300 km across the Caspian in about a month by "floatimming", then the Channel in 3 days (~20 h in the water) because Eurotunnel wouldn't let him walk the tunnel (rows 11, 32–34, 37–39).
3. **Pause, fly out, come back to the exact spot.** Visas gave him 90 days in 180; each time he left, he returned to where he had stopped. Only about half of the 28 years was spent walking (rows 10, 13, 23).
4. **Russia alone took nearly 10 years.** He was arrested the day he stepped off the Bering ice (no entry stamp). When Russia refused a visa, he walked about 5,000 km across America to the Russian embassy to protest (rows 18, 21, 22, 25).
5. **10,229 days, ~75 million steps, 10 pairs of shoes in the Americas before he lost count.** It started with a birthday card and a few hundred dollars (rows 64–66, 6, 4).

- **Strongest human story:** the son he left at 8 is now 36, and he met his 12-year-old granddaughter for the first time at the door (row 55), with "I'm about to lose my reason to live" (row 58).
- **Best visual image:** one continuous line, drawn by a cart called "The Beast", from Cape Horn up through the Americas, over the frozen Bering Strait ("like Moses watching the Red Sea part", row 19) and across Asia and Europe. It ends at a garden gate in Hull with a banner on it. Alternative: crossing the Humber Bridge in thick fog the day before (row 43).
- **For the 「你」/photo format:** he left with a cheap plastic film camera and came home with a smartphone (rows 4, 67). No source counts his photos.
