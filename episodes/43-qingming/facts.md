## Corrections to the brief (summary)
1. **1922 → 1923**: the scroll left the Forbidden City in January 1923 (宣统十四年十一月十八日 = 1923-01-04; Palace Museum centenary text says 1923). C24.
2. **徽宗瘦金体 title**: recorded by 杨准 (1352) and 李东阳, but **no longer on the scroll** (cut off by a 明末 mounter, per 余辉). Never say "画上有徽宗题字". C3.
3. **嘉靖 confiscation into the palace**: the Palace Museum page dates the entry into the palace to **隆庆**; use no year. C19.
4. **朱文徵, 徐文靖** as owners: not supported by what I could open. C14.
5. **王忬/汤臣/《金瓶梅》**: legend (相传), already refuted as the cause of 王忬's death by 吴晗. C17–18.
6. **Size 528.7 cm** is one of two Palace Museum figures (528 / 528.7); **counts** differ (500余 / 约600 / 814). O1–O2.
7. **Transfer to the Palace Museum**: 1953 / 1955 / 1957 / 1958 all appear; say "1950年代". C32.
8. **1945**: no reliable record of where the scroll was seized. C28.
9. **杨仁恺 "就是这个！"**: journalist's narration, not his documented words. C30.

## Story-worthy (ranked; each with a candidate fate-teaser line and its payoff row)

1. **四出五进**: in ~900 years it was carried out of the palace 4 times and came back 5 times (Palace Museum's own phrase). Teaser: "你4次被带出皇宫" → C5, C9, C20, C24, C32. This is the spine of the film and the "handover set".
2. **The swap**: a Yuan palace mounter replaced you with a copy and sold you (杨准, 1352). Teaser: **"你被人偷偷换成了假的"** → C9–C11.
3. **The emperor who stole from himself**: the last emperor smuggled you out as a "gift to his brother", within less than a month of three other scrolls with your name (1922-12-30 → 1923-01-24). Teaser: **"你被皇帝自己偷运出宫"** → C24–C25.
4. **Found in the junk pile**: 1950, a temporary storeroom in 沈阳, three scrolls with your name; a young researcher picks the battered one. Teaser: **"你躺在一堆破画里"** → C30–C31. (Strongest human story, see below.)
5. **The eunuch**: 1578, 冯保 writes his name on you, then walks you out of the palace. Teaser: **"一个太监把你带出了皇宫"** → C20.
6. **The donkey that never existed**: a Ming mounter misread a broken shed as donkey ears and painted a donkey onto you; in 1973 the Palace Museum peeled it off. Teaser: "有人在你身上画了一头驴" → C33. (Mirrors the film's theme: fakes on the real thing.)
7. **Hidden at a friend's house**: 陆完 hid you before his house was raided; his mother died in prison. Teaser: "你被藏进了朋友家" → C15.
8. **Confiscated into the palace (twice)**: 严家 fell → palace; 毕沅 died → his house raided → 嘉庆's five seals. Teaser: "你被抄家，抄进了皇宫" → C19, C22–C23.
9. **The queue**: 2005, 14 days and nearly empty rooms; 2015, 13-hour queues and closing at 4 a.m.; 2025, 4–5 hours for 1–2 minutes. Teaser / present-day: **"排队5小时，只为看你1分钟"** → P1–P3.
10. **Your name was cut off**: 徽宗's five-character title slip and his dragon seal, recorded twice, trimmed away by a mounter. Teaser: "你的名字，被人裁掉了" → C3.

- **Best hook fact:** 四出五进 (O6), paired with the paradox of 张著's first line "藏者宜宝之" (C6): the very first person to write on you told every future owner to treasure you; almost none of them could keep you.
- **Strongest human story:** 杨仁恺, 1950: a researcher at a museum barely a year old (C29) goes through Puyi's confiscated scrolls in a temporary storeroom, finds three called 清明上河图, and picks the torn one, checking it against 《东京梦华录》 and the 1186 colophon (C30–C31). Runner-up: 冯忠莲 copying you for nearly ten years, interrupted by the Cultural Revolution (C34).
- **Best visual image:** the scroll passing in and out of the same palace gate (四出五进) while the red seals pile up on its tail (96 seals, O5) and the age counter runs; finale: the 午门 queue snaking up the ramp at midnight (P3) for one or two minutes in front of a 5-metre scroll.

## Risks

- **Legends:** 王忬/黄彪/汤臣 and the 《金瓶梅》 revenge story are 相传 (C17–18); the brief already bans presenting them as fact. If used at all: one line, stamped "相传（明人笔记）". The Yuan swap (C9) rests on one man's colophon. Stamp it "（杨准跋）" and it is fine. 冯保's "theft" is the Palace Museum's own wording (偷盗); his method is unknown.
- **Contested numbers:** date painted, size, figure counts, transfer year, discovery year, 1945 path. Use the "holds for all" wordings in C1, O1–O2, C28, C30, C32.
- **Attribution:** the painting is unsigned; 张择端 rests on one colophon (C2, C6). Fine to say "张择端画的", since the Palace Museum does, but don't claim more about his life than C6/O7 give.
- **伪满 / 溥仪 (political sensitivity):** use 伪满, 伪满皇宫 (mainland official usage) every time; never "满洲国皇帝" without 伪. Describe 1923 as the Palace Museum does (偷运出宫 / "以赏赐的名义"). Keep Puyi factual and not sympathetic or comic. 1945: the Soviet capture of Puyi is fact (C27); don't claim the Soviets took the scroll (unproven, C28). Official framing (CCTV 2025: "在党和国家的关怀下，才得以重返故里") is available if the producer wants an official line. Using it is not required.
- **Taiwan:** say 台北故宫博物院 (not 国立故宫博物院); no flags or emblems; present the 清院本 neutrally as "另一卷".
- **Maps:** owner rule: no maps with borders. If showing the route 汴京 → 金 → 元宫 → 泰和 → 苏州 → 北京 → 长春 → 大栗子 → 沈阳 → 北京, use city dots on an abstract ground, no borders or territory shapes (Jin/Song/Manchukuo borders are exactly the kind that trigger review).
- **Real people:** 杨仁恺 (1915–2008) and his family; 冯忠莲; living Palace Museum staff (余辉, 朱鸿文, 徐婉玲, 祝勇). Quote only what is in the rows. No invented dialogue presented as quotation (e.g. "就是这个！").
- **Platform:** Douyin fact-checks (抖音小安). Every on-screen stamp must map to a row here. Image licence (L1–L2): no Palace Museum photos or logo without written permission. Declare any AI content (the brief bans AI images). The 2026 Suzhou showing (P7) is a real event; a "2026 rumour" headline about the scroll being fake would be invented (P8), so don't date it.
- **Tone:** 陆完's mother dying in prison and 王忬's execution are deaths. Handle them like ep rules: dim, no depiction.
