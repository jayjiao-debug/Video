## Corrections to the brief (read first)

1. **「宋朝人也点外卖」 is fair only in a narrow sense.** What the Song texts I could open actually show:
   - city people buying cooked food at shops instead of cooking (S1);
   - **calling wine from a shop, which sends it in silver vessels and collects them next day** (S2);
   - hiring a whole banquet service to your house (S7);
   - restaurants taking orders at any hour (S4);
   - runners who fetched things for drinkers (S5);
   - a tavern that sent a man out to buy food from other sellers (S6).
   They do **not** show an app, a menu you phone, a named delivery job, a fee, or a 食盒 in the delivery sense. **Explicit "order dishes in the city, they are carried to you" first shows up in 清 扬州 (Q1, ⚠ second-hand).**
   - Safest hook: **「宋朝人，也‘叫’过外卖：叫的是酒。」** (S2). Frame 0 from the brief, 「你点的外卖，宋朝人也点过。」, is acceptable only if the next beat names what: wine/food called from shops.
2. **「凡百所卖饮食之人……」 is not about delivery.** In 《东京梦华录》 卷五 it says food sellers keep their trays and vessels clean and pretty (S12). The 外卖 articles that use it as a delivery quote are wrong. Don't use it as delivery evidence.
3. **The word 「外卖」 does appear in 《东京梦华录》, once** (卷二 饮食果子), in a list of foods peddlers sold to drinkers in taverns (S5). It means "sold from outside", not "delivered". The nearby 「外买」 (卷四 会仙酒楼) is the tavern sending someone out to buy (S6). A fact-checker who looks up 「外卖」 will find this; the on-screen wording should not claim "宋朝人发明了外卖这个词" in the modern sense.
4. **「闲汉」 were not "the first professional couriers".** The definition (S5) is hangers-on in taverns who ran errands for young drinkers: buy things, call singers, carry money. Press pieces that call them 「最早的专职外卖员」 (曾庆江, 海南日报 2020) go beyond the text.
5. **「索唤」 is not a delivery word.** It is "to call for / order". In the Song texts it is used for diners ordering dishes in a shop, for buying meat at a stall, and for the emperor calling for things (F2).
6. **Emperors "ordering takeout".** Texts I could check: 北宋 元宵 vendors laid out food 「准备御前索唤」 (S9, ✓); 南宋 the emperor's boat 「宣唤」 lake vendors and 宋五嫂 got rich (N3, ⚠ second-hand 《武林旧事》). The specific stories (宋孝宗 买 李婆杂菜羹 etc.; 宋太祖 令市脯; 「直一贯者，犒之二贯」) are UNVERIFIED (F1).
7. **Platform numbers disagree by source.** 新华网 (2025-01) says 5.45亿 users; CNNIC says 5.92亿 at Dec 2024 and 6.30亿 at Dec 2025 (T1). Use only CNNIC's.
8. **Nothing found for 1950-1980s.** I could not find a sourced delivery practice for those decades. Don't invent a 单位食堂 or 粮票 beat as "delivery". Skip from 民国 to the 1990s.
9. **Years are rounded.** 「北宋 · 约1100年」 and 「南宋 · 约1180年」 are 估算 (see section I).

## J. Story-worthy

**The 5 most surprising facts:**
1. **Wine came in borrowed silver.** 《东京梦华录》: even poor households that called wine from a shop got it in silver vessels, and the shop collected them the next day (S2). A shop would lend hundreds of 两 of silver to a pub after two or three purchases (S3).
2. **The book's only 「外卖」 is a peddler's list**, not delivery (S5), and even a tavern with a silver-bowl-for-one-drinker policy sent a man out to buy the side dishes (S6).
3. **Vendors waited, laid out in dense rows, "to be called by the emperor"** at the Lantern Festival (S9). A Southern Song emperor on a boat called a refugee's fish soup; she got rich (N3, ⚠).
4. **Households that kept no vegetables at home** (S1) and a night market that stayed open to 三更 and reopened at 五更 (S14).
5. **扬州 "订菜"**: order at a city restaurant; every evening the dishes are sent along the embankment to each boat (Q1, ⚠).
- Runner-up: the tea-kettle man who also checks on neighbours (S15). Another: 6.30亿 people (56.0% of netizens) use online delivery today (T1).

**Strongest human story:** **宋五嫂**: a woman who came south with the court after the fall of the old capital, sold fish soup on West Lake, was called by the emperor, and "became rich" (N3). It must stay ⚠ and be told as 「《武林旧事》记载」. Second choice: the unnamed 闲汉 in the tavern, doing small errands for tips (S5).

**Best visual image:** the **silver bowl**: a Song hand holds a silver bowl of wine, a hand takes it back the next day (S2). The bowl then becomes a bamboo cage, then a phone and a paper bag that no one returns (I4). Second: the emperor's red-lit viewing stand with rows of snack stalls packed in front (S9).
