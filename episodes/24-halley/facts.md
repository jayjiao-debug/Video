## Story-worthy

**Five most surprising facts**
1. **618 days, split by planet.** Jupiter delays the comet 518 days and Saturn 100 (#19). Two invisible pulls push a date back by almost two years, and three people worked it out by hand.
2. **The prediction landed about 6 weeks before the sighting.** Clairaut announced mid-April 1759 to the Académie on 14 Nov 1758 (#22). Palitzsch saw the comet on 25 Dec (#14). It reached perihelion on 13 Mar 1759, about a month early but inside the stated ±1 month (#17, #20, #21).
3. **Halley softened his own words.** In Latin (1705) he would "confidently predict" 1758. The English said "I dare venture to foretell", and by 1715 it was "I think I may venture to foretell" (#7). Over time he hedged more, not less.
4. **The "same" comet doesn't keep the same schedule.** Its period has run from 74.4 to 79.3 years (#26), because Jupiter stretched the next lap after a close pass in 1681 (#27, #9). Halley already blamed Jupiter for the unequal gaps in 1705.
5. **Chinese records cover about 2,200 years.** The 240 BC entry in 《史记》 (#28–29) starts a "nearly unbroken" series (#30), and modern orbit work was checked against it (Yeomans: the model "successfully represented all the existing Chinese observations").

**Strongest human story**
Halley published "I dare venture to foretell… 1758" when he was 48 (#7, #11). He died in 1742, 16 years too early (#12, #13). In Paris, three people, one of them a woman later left out of the book (#24), spent six months "from morning to night, sometimes even at meals" (#23) recomputing his comet. Then on Christmas night a Saxon farmer looking through his own telescope (#14, #15) found it first. Nobody who did the maths saw it first. For the callback: the viewer can work out their own age at the 28 July 2061 return (#39).

**Best visual image**
A ledger of three dates, 1531, 1607 and 1682, with a 76/75 gap that doesn't quite match (#3, #9). Jupiter's pull appears as a weight dragging a dot along an ellipse: +518 days, then Saturn +100 days (#19). The calendar slides from April 1759 back to 13 March 1759. Alternative end image: the Westminster Abbey slate comet with Giotto in its head (#40).

## Risks

- **Brief's own numbers to correct:** "Clairaut's correction of ~618 days" is right only as a **delay vs. an unperturbed period** (518 Jupiter + 100 Saturn). Don't call it the error of Halley's prediction. The final prediction was **mid-April 1759 ±1 month**; the actual perihelion was **13 March 1759**. Don't put a single exact predicted day on screen (13 vs 15 April in the sources).
- **Disputed / unverified, do not use:** the "impartial posterity… an Englishman" quote (#10, probably 1749, not 1705); 《春秋》613 BC as Halley (#32); 1057 BC / 武王伐纣 (#33); "self-made telescope" (#16). Linda Hall Library's "1535" and "1911" are errors. The St Andrews page's "13 April" actual perihelion is an error.
- **"Earliest record" claims:** say 「目前能确认的最早记录」, not 「世界最早」. Greek (466 BC) and Babylonian claims exist, and nationalistic framing ("中国最早发现") invites fact-check pushback.
- **Chinese original wording:** the 《史记》 text here comes from a Japanese scholarly quotation. Check the exact characters against the 中华书局点校本 before they go on screen.
- **Real people:** Halley, Palitzsch, Clairaut, Lalande, Lepaute (all 18th-c., no likeness rights issue). Portraits should be stylised 2D figures, not copied from paintings. Lepaute's omission (#24) is a secondary-source claim; if used, phrase it neutrally (「书中没有写她的名字」).
- **Maps:** none are needed. If the film shows Dresden/Paris/London, use a borderless star-map or a dot on a blank landmass (brief: no national borders).
- **Dates/calendars:** Halley's death date is Old Style (14 Jan = 25 Jan Gregorian). Show only the year. BC dates: 前240年 (astronomers' year −239).
- **1910 panic:** don't exaggerate it into mass hysteria or suicides (some popular claims are unsourced). Galea/SHI support "comet pills" and the cyanogen scare only.
- **2061 date:** the exact day comes from a 2025 arXiv preprint. NASA confirms the year. If a platform asks, cite NASA for the year and Barbieri et al. for the day.
- **Platform:** low risk. No deaths, politics or health claims. Avoid astrology/omen framing ("彗星=灾星") beyond historical description.
