# Brief: 人活着为了什么？人类问了四千年 (ep42, working title)

- **Owner ask (2026-10-24 12:31):** "something that tells history, different dates, places etc, advancements maybe like, The first person to think about the meaning of life, then we trace back to many ancients etc"
- **Format:** the 《为了跟你说一句话，人类努力了三千年》 engine: a relay across ~4,000 years.
  - Each stop is a date, a place and a person, labelled on screen, with narration in the place's own language where TTS allows, plus Chinese subtitles.
  - One-tone silhouettes, seamless motivated transitions, a bookend open and close, music-mapped.
- **Hook:** 「人类历史上，第一个问"人活着到底为了什么"的人，是谁？」
- **Bookend:** open today, 3 a.m.: someone types 「人活着到底为了什么」 into a phone. Close on the oldest written answer, which turns out to be the newest one.
- **Relay (all to verify; drop any that can't be sourced):**
  1. **c. 1800 BC, Babylonia.** Gilgamesh, after Enkidu dies, searches for a way not to die. The ale-wife Siduri tells him: eat, dance, wash, cherish the child who holds your hand, let your wife delight in your embrace, "this is the lot of man" (Old Babylonian version, the Sippar/Meissner tablet).
  2. **c. 1900 BC, Egypt.** *The Dispute between a Man and his Ba*: a man weary of life argues with his own soul.
  3. **c. 5th c. BC, India.** Siddhartha's four sights (old age, sickness, death, a renunciant). He leaves the palace.
  4. **c. 500 BC, Lu.** Confucius: 未知生，焉知死 / 朝闻道，夕死可矣.
  5. **399 BC, Athens.** Socrates at his trial: "the unexamined life is not worth living".
  6. **c. 4th c. BC.** Zhuangzi drums on a basin and sings when his wife dies (鼓盆而歌).
  7. **Ecclesiastes** (date uncertain): 虚空的虚空.
  8. **c. AD 170s, the Danube front.** Marcus Aurelius writes the *Meditations* to himself in his tent.
  9. **1670 (posthumous), France.** Pascal: man is a thinking reed.
  10. **1889, Germany.** Nietzsche: "He who has a why to live can bear almost any how."
  11. **1946, Vienna.** Viktor Frankl writes *Man's Search for Meaning* after the camps. He says meaning comes from work, love, and the attitude toward suffering.
  12. **1938 → today, Harvard Study of Adult Development.** Relationships are the strongest predictor of a happy, healthy life.
- **Takeaway:** we have asked for 4,000 years. The first written answer (Siduri: the hand you hold, the people you love) and the longest modern study land in the same place.
- **Comment question:** 「你活着，是为了谁？」
