# Cold read v1: 42-yiyi (subtitles only, lines.json)

Viewer: 20, China, scrolling Douyin in the evening. I read only `lines.json` (35 lines, 0 to 151 s). I could not see any visuals, so anything that depends on the screen is marked "if the screen shows it". Line numbers below are 1-based in file order (#1 = 「凌晨三点…」, #35 = 「你活着，是为了[谁]？」).

## 1. Did the first 3 s keep me?
Yes, but only just, and it depends on the screen.
- #1 「凌晨三点，你也搜过这句吧？」: "3 a.m." gets me right away. The problem is 「这句」. Nothing in the subtitles tells me which sentence. If the screen shows a search box with something like 「人活着有什么意义」, it works. If it doesn't, I have to guess what "this sentence" is.
- What actually hooks me is #2 and #3 (3 to 9 s): 「近四千年前…有人写下了它」 and then 「泥板上的答案，和[哈佛]一样」. Putting a clay tablet next to Harvard is a weird pair, and I want to know how they match. That's the reason I stay.

## 2. Where I would swipe away
1. **9.25 to 16.66 s: 7.4 s with no subtitle.** The hook has just promised an answer, and then nothing happens. Unless the picture or music is very strong, this is my biggest swipe point. A third of a 20-second attention window is empty.
2. **82 to 92 s (#20 to #21, Nietzsche).** By now I've had Confucius, Socrates, an unnamed emperor and Nietzsche, all quotes, one after another. It starts to feel like a 名人名言串烧 (a medley of famous quotes). The only thing keeping me here is the Gilgamesh open loop from #10 (「留到最后」).
3. **135.11 to 140.86 s: a 5.75 s gap after #32 「三千多年前的答案，和哈佛一样」.** That line sounds like the ending. I'd probably swipe here and miss the hotpot line, which is the best line for someone my age.
4. Lower risk at **46 to 56 s (#11 to #13, Confucius).** I learned 未知生焉知死 in school, so it feels like a textbook moment. The loop from #10 still holds me.

## 3. Lines I didn't understand or had to reread
- **#1 「这句」**: which sentence? (See above.)
- **#4 「一个无名诗人」**: where and when was this? Right after 「近四千年前…有人写下了它」, I assumed this poet wrote the clay tablet. Then Gilgamesh shows up and I wasn't sure which text is "the tablet". It feels like one text but seems to be two.
- **#2 vs #32 (the numbers don't match)**: the opening says 「近四千年前」 and the ending says 「三千多年前」. I noticed this. Was it 4,000 years or 3,000? It makes the payoff feel slightly off.
- **#14 「只差[30票]就能无罪」**: an interesting fact, but then what? The line never says he was executed, so the "30 votes" just hangs there as trivia without a point.
- **#17 to #19 (the emperor)**: the quote arrives before I know who's speaking, and he's never named. 「管着帝国的人」: which empire? I worked out Marcus Aurelius only because I've heard of 《沉思录》. Most people my age won't. The joke still works, but I had to reread.
- **#18 「做一个人该做的事」**: vague. What is "the work of a human being"? It sounds deep but gives me nothing concrete.
- **#24 「获救后他才知道，妻子已经不在了」**: this hit me hard, but I didn't know what to take from it. Did the love save him even though she was already dead? Or did love fail? It cuts straight to Harvard 1.8 s later, so there's no time to think about it.
- **#26 「他们原以为，50岁的胆固醇更要紧」**: who are 「他们」, the researchers? 「50岁的胆固醇」 is clumsy wording. I read it twice to get "cholesterol levels at age 50".
- **#27 「看的是[关系]」**: on its own, 关系 makes me think of 走关系/靠关系 (pulling strings). First reaction: "so being healthy and happy when you're old depends on connections, lol." 人际关系 or 亲密关系 would remove the joke reading.
- **#31 「这，就是人的命运」**: 命运 sounds fatalistic and grand. Is she saying this is our fate, or this is what we're supposed to do? It's slightly unclear.
- **#33 「你也没睡？明天吃火锅！」**: it uses the same 「」 brackets as the historical quotes, so for half a second I wondered who said it. I get it if the screen shows a chat bubble.

## 5. Does the ending pay off 「泥板上的答案，和哈佛一样」?
**Mostly yes.** #28 to #31 bring back the tavern keeper's line, and #32 says outright that it is the same as Harvard. Child and wife on one side, "relationships" on the other: the match is clear and satisfying. Three things weaken it:
1. The 近四千年 vs 三千多年 mismatch (#2 vs #32).
2. The tablet answer is a child and a wife, and I'm 20 with neither. My honest first reaction was "我连对象都没有". The hotpot line (#33) saves this by widening it to friends. It's the line that makes the answer about me, so it shouldn't come after a 5.75 s gap that feels like the end.
3. I wasn't sure which tablet 「泥板」 refers to (see #4).

## 6. Preachy, cheesy or unclear
- **#23 「人的救赎，[在于爱]」**: 救赎 is heavy and sounds like a translated book. Slightly preachy.
- **#34 「答案不在世界尽头，[就在身边]」**: chicken-soup (鸡汤) wording. The callback to 「世界尽头」 earns it a bit, but it's the line most likely to get an eye-roll.
- **#35 「你活着，是为了[谁]？」**: switching from "why" to "for whom" is clever, but it can read like a guilt trip (「我活着都是为了你」, the line parents use). It could also start an argument in the comments: "为什么要为了谁？我为自己活。" That's good bait for comments, but risky.
- **#31 「这，就是人的命运」**: grand and a bit vague (see above).
- **#10 「她后面的话，[留到最后]」**: openly a retention trick, but it works on me. Keep it.
