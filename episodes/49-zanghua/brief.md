# ep49 brief: 为什么你听得出哪个是脏话？ (2026-10-25)

## Why this episode is different
The owner, after looking at his analytics: "I'm just copying others. I want to do something unique, where people can feel the
passion and the thinking, and memorable for someone." The new format (docs/ideas.md):
- A simple question people have half-wondered about and never looked up.
- **The viewer takes part first**: they listen, guess and commit. Then the film shows why they already "knew".
- Narrated, with **real recorded sound effects** (no generated SFX unless approved). Subtitles stay on screen.
- No borrowed frames: no 「假如你是X」 and no copying another account's look.

## The idea (owner's words)
"If I play multiple clips in different languages of people speaking and swearing, most people could identify the swearing
clips. Why? Hook: listen to these 2 sound clips… which one do you think is swearing? Most people would get it right, but why?"

## What research changed (facts.md; use only ✓ rows on screen, safe wording given there)
- **The twist is real.** Lev-Ari & McKay 2022 played people pairs of *invented* words in one calm machine voice. Every word was made up, yet people picked the one without l/r/w/y as "the swear word" on 63 % of trials (chance 50 %). 37 of the 215 participants were Chinese speakers. The paper's own Chinese pairs were gou → rou/chou and kai → lai/chai.
  So the film can run the real experiment on the viewer, with invented words, and reveal: 「这两个词，都不存在。」
- **Two rounds** separate "you heard the anger" (tone, which people recognise across cultures) from "you heard the word" (sound).
- **Platform:** no real swear word may be audible or spelled out, in any language, and no bleep gags (网综细则 §30; 抖音 rules on vulgar speech). Invented words only, which is also the science.
- **Caveats (must respect):**
  - 63 % is a share of trials, not "most people were right".
  - Real swear-word counts were not significant after correction.
  - Plosives were *not* over-represented.
  - Mandarin speakers were among the few groups below chance on bouba/kiki.
  - Nothing shows Chinese 脏话 follow the pattern.
  - Swearing helps people *hold on* in ice water (only real swear words; made-up ones didn't); it doesn't kill pain.

## Format
- 16:9, 1920×1080, about 2:00–2:40.
- Mandarin narration (ElevenLabs, native standard voice, picked by the owner) and subtitles.
- Real-recording sound design. Music bed from the owner (ask).
- Interactive beats: a countdown or choice card where the viewer picks A or B before the answer. Leave real pauses: let the viewer guess.
- Ending: a question that sends the viewer back to their own mouth or ears (they say a word out loud, notice something), and an invitation to comment their guess.
