## Story-worthy

**The 5 most surprising facts**
1. The bomb tests of the 1950s–60s roughly **doubled** the carbon-14 in the air and in every living thing (#37). The "clock" was knocked off by humans, and that spike is now itself a precise clock for anything after 1950 (#39).
2. Laboratories still compute every "radiocarbon age" with Libby's **wrong** half-life, 5568 years, on purpose, so that old and new dates stay comparable (#27). Libby's own 1949 test paper used 5720 ± 47, which is closer to today's value.
3. "Present" in every radiocarbon date is frozen at **1950** (#24), the last year before bomb carbon spoiled the baseline.
4. Coal burning had already diluted atmospheric C-14 by about **2.5%** by the 1950s (#36). A plant grown in 1950 already "looked" older than it was.
5. The first Chinese lab proved itself in 1965 the same way Libby did in 1949: with a **blind test on wood of known age** (four samples from 佟柱臣) before anyone trusted it (#13 ↔ #17).

**Strongest human story:** Libby's 1949 gamble. The idea predicts the age of pharaoh-era wood (Zoser at Sakkara, Sneferu at Meydum, ~4,600 years) from nothing but a decay count, and the paper's verdict is one dry line: "The agreement between prediction and observation is seen to be satisfactory." (#17–20). Chinese mirror: in 1959 Xia Nai hands two young nuclear physicists, Qiu Shihua and Cai Lianzhen, Libby's book (#12); in 1965 they pass the blind test (#13); from 1972, the year Mawangdui opened, they publish dates every year (#10).

**Best visual image:** a slice of wood whose rings are a calendar you can count one by one (#34), laid beside a sample that "ticks" ~15 times a minute per gram when alive (#23) and slows by half every ~5,700 years. Then one ring from the 1960s glows twice as bright: the bomb ring (#37).

## Risks

- **Mawangdui C-14 result not verified (#7).** The brief requires "what was dated, by which lab, results", and I could not find it online. The tomb's exact date comes from written evidence (tomb 3 tablet, seals), not C-14 (#8, also UNVERIFIED). Recommended framing until a library check: Mawangdui opened in 1972, the same year China's first C-14 lab began publishing annual dates (#10, #11). Do not claim "C-14 dated Mawangdui to X".
- **The 76.7% figure (#9)** is a school exercise, not a measurement. Showing it would break both the owner's "no textbook" rule and the "no invented numbers" rule. Use 示意 numbers from #30 instead.
- **Human remains:** the Mawangdui woman (辛追) is a real person whose body is on display. No close-ups of the corpse, no "mummy horror" framing; show the tomb, coffins or lacquerware instead.
- **Real people:** Libby, Arnold, Qiu Shihua, Cai Lianzhen, Xia Nai and 佟柱臣 are real. Use the 2D figure system without likeness claims, and don't invent dialogue.
- **Nuclear tests:** keep it abstract (a curve, a glowing tree ring). No mushroom clouds or country flags. That avoids platform sensitivity and keeps the film from being about weapons.
- **Maps:** none needed. If places appear (Sakkara, Meydum, Changsha), use a dot plus a label, never borders.
- **Pseudo-science magnet:** the comments may pull in young-earth or "C-14 is fake" arguments. Calibration (#33–35) is the honest answer. Avoid any wording that implies dates are "only guesses".
- **Half-life numbers disagree** (5568 / 5700 / 5730): use "约5730年" or "大约5700多年", never mix them in one calculation without saying which.
