# Script: 走进房间就忘了 (ep33) · first draft (producer, no crew yet)

**Logline:** You walk into the kitchen and forget why. In a lab, walking through a doorway roughly tripled one kind of memory error compared with walking the same distance in one room. The brain cuts life into "episodes", and a door tells it the last one is over. But a 2021 re-run found the door alone barely matters: it bites when your head is already busy.
**Takeaway:** 让你忘事的不只是门，是满脑子的事撞上了一扇门。
**Length:** ~100 s · 33 lines, all ≤ 16 字 · 1920×1080, subtitle-only, ~2 s Juno end card.
**发给谁 / end-card question:** 你今天在门口忘过什么？

## Title options
1. **进门就忘** (4 字): short, says the everyday moment, works as a cover.
2. **走进房间就忘了** (7 字): the working title; most literal.
3. **门会偷走记忆？** (6 字): the hook as a question; a little clickbait, and the film's twist partly answers "no".

## Set-piece (one take)
First-person camera walks through one flat for the whole film: hallway → kitchen → back through the hallway → bedroom. The thing you came for (a glowing outline of a cup / key) travels with the camera and gets "left behind" each time the frame crosses a door frame, like a page flipping. The lab scenes happen *inside* the same flat: the rooms turn into the experiment's game rooms (grey tables, coloured blocks), so there's no hard cut to a laboratory.

## Facts (first pass, producer; the researcher still has to confirm against the PDFs)
| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Virtual-environment task: pick up an object at a table, carry it to another table, set it down, pick up a new one; same distance either within a room or through a doorway. | Radvansky & Copeland (2006) *Memory & Cognition* 34(5) 1150–1156, doi:10.3758/BF03193261; Radvansky, Krawietz & Tamplin (2011) *QJEP* 64(8) 1632–1645, doi:10.1080/17470218.2011.571267 | abstract read |
| F2 | 2011 Exp. 1 (55 students, VR): error rate on negative probes 22% after a room shift vs 7% without; positive probes 19% vs 12%. | Radvansky et al. 2011, as reported by memory-key.com | **to verify in the paper** (secondary source) |
| F3 | 2011 Exp. 2 (60 students, real rooms, shoeboxes): smaller effect, positive 28% vs 23%, negative 21% vs 18%. | same | **to verify** |
| F4 | Event-model / situation-model account: a new location is an event boundary; information from the previous event becomes less available. | Radvansky & Copeland 2006 abstract; Radvansky et al. 2011 | abstract read |
| F5 | 2011 Exp. 3: going back into the original room did not restore memory (context-matching alone doesn't explain it). | Radvansky et al. 2011 (via the Scientific American write-up) | **to verify** |
| F6 | McFadyen et al. (2021) *BMC Psychology* 9:41, doi:10.1186/s40359-021-00536-3 (Bond University): with no extra load, doorways had no significant effect (VR and real hallway); with a backward-counting task, sensitivity fell after doorways, driven by more false alarms (≈37.9% vs 30.1%). | paper PDF read | verified |
