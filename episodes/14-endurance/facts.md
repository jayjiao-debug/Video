## Story-worthy

**5 most surprising facts**
1. They went **497 days** without setting foot on land (5 Dec 1914 → 15 Apr 1916) — RGS's own count; it checks out to the day.
2. They tried to walk to land across the ice and stopped: at that pace "it would take us over three hundred days to reach the land" [SOUTH Ch. VI] — the giant goal was dropped for a smaller, reachable one (Patience Camp, wait for the ice to open). This supports the brief's thesis, using Shackleton's own words.
3. The wreck was found **on 5 March 2022, 100 years to the day after Shackleton was buried**, only ~4 miles from where navigator Frank Worsley had fixed her position with a sextant in 1915.
4. Shackleton saw what he thought was the sky clearing; it was "the white crest of an enormous wave", the biggest in his 26 years at sea, and the 20-ft boat survived it.
5. Rescue took **four attempts** in three different countries' ships before a small Chilean navy tug, *Yelcho* (Luis Pardo), got through. The 22 men waited 4½ months under two upturned boats, eating seal and penguin. Every one of the 28 survived.

**Strongest human story:** the 22 men left on Elephant Island under Frank Wild, living under two upturned boats for 4½ months with no way to know if the 20-ft boat had made it, and the moment of rescue: "All safe, all well." Second choice: the stern. The last thing the men saw was the stern going down ("She went down bows first, her stern raised in the air"), and 106 years later the first thing the lights found clearly was the name and Polaris star on that same stern. That gives a natural bookend for the film.

**Best visual image:** the dive. A light moves through black water at 3,008 m and finds the letters E-N-D-U-R-A-N-C-E arced across the stern with the five-pointed star under them, "just as in Hurley's photographs". Next best: the tiny James Caird in front of a wave mistaken for a break in the clouds. Also good: the ship held upright in pressure ice in polar night (Hurley's flash photo, recreated in 3D, not copied).

## Risks

- **Maps / borders:** South Georgia and the Falklands are disputed (UK/Argentina); Antarctic territorial claims overlap. Use a borderless ocean/ice map with no country fills or labels for South Georgia's sovereignty, and no claim lines in Antarctica. Label places only: 威德尔海, 象岛, 南乔治亚岛. Avoid "英属" / "Malvinas"-type labels.
- **"Lone heroic leader" framing:** the brief bans it. Credit the crew, Worsley's navigation, Pardo and the Chilean navy, and the 22 who held on.
- **Overclaiming:** never "全员生还" / "无一人死亡" for the whole expedition. Three Ross Sea party men died [SPRI-RSP]. Say "坚忍号上的28人全部生还". Don't invent wave heights, temperatures or quotes. Don't state "7米" or "1300公里" as exact figures (see rows 20, 21). Use "100多年" rather than 106/107.
- **Real people:** all named men are real and long dead. Show them only as stylised, unnamed silhouettes or 2D characters, use no invented dialogue, and attribute quotes to their source (South, 1919). Dogs being shot and Blackborow's amputation must stay off-screen and implied.
- **Frank Hurley's photos:** the original Endurance negatives are held by the **Royal Geographical Society (with IBG)**, London ("nearly 150 glass plates and 39 celluloid negatives", [NATGEO]; [RGS brochure](https://www.rgs.org/media/nznfgfd0/endurance-platinum-print-new-brochure-for-web-2026-updated-pricing.pdf)). Prints and other material are also at SLNSW and SPRI. Copyright status depends on the country. SLNSW marked the one Hurley record we opened "Out of copyright: Please acknowledge: Mitchell Library, State Library of New South Wales". That record is from Mawson's 1911–14 expedition, so check each Endurance item's own record. In the UK, life+70 (Hurley died 1962) could run to the end of 2032, and the RGS licenses these images commercially. **Recommendation:** use no Hurley photos. Recreate the scenes in 3D instead. If a photo is ever needed, use only an SLNSW "out of copyright" item with the credit line, after the owner approves.
- **2022 wreck footage and stills:** "Image © Falklands Maritime Heritage Trust / National Geographic" [E22-PR]. These are copyrighted, so do not use them. Recreate the stern and the name in 3D.
- **Fake recruitment ad** ("Men wanted for hazardous journey… safe return doubtful"): no original has ever been found. SPRI's Bob Headland: "I'm fairly convinced this haystack doesn't have a needle in it". Its earliest known appearance is a 1949 book that dates it to 1900 [SMITH]. **Never use it, not even as a "myth" aside.**
- **Platform:** no gore or animal cruelty on screen. Antarctic Treaty protection means we must not imply the wreck was touched or raised.
- **Open / UNVERIFIED:** the ats.aq HSM 93 listing page was not opened (UKAHT and E22 agree on it). The 22.5 ft James Caird length has no allowed source. The miles in "800 miles" may be nautical or statute. The exact date Mackintosh and Hayward died is unconfirmed. The Gutenberg text could only be read through Ch. II via the fetch tool, so Ch. III–XII were read in the Standard Ebooks copy of the same public-domain text.
