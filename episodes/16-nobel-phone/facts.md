## Story-worthy

**5 most surprising facts**
1. **The ECB's "bazooka" was never fired** (rows 31–36). One sentence on 26 Jul 2012, a programme announced 6 weeks later, and by Jan 2015 the EU's top court was told it "has not been implemented in any specific case". Spain's 10-year yield still fell from a 6.80% monthly average (Jul 2012) to 5.34% (Dec 2012). Peer-reviewed event study (row 54–55): on 26 Jul alone, Spanish/Italian 2-year yields fell 77/83 bp; the three OMT announcement dates together cut them by about 2 percentage points while German/French yields did not move. The ECB later told the EU court the mere announcement was enough (row 57).
2. **The Fed changed nothing but a date** (rows 26–28). On 9 Aug 2011 the rate was already near zero. The FOMC only added "at least through mid-2013", and two-year market rates "immediately" fell to "barely above 10 basis points". Woodford, the new laureate, analysed this himself, chart by chart.
3. **Words can backfire** (rows 37–40). In 2013 the Fed only hinted at slowing bond purchases, mostly as analysts expected, and the 10-year yield went from 1.76% (Apr) to 2.81% (Sep). Mortgage rates "increased significantly". Same tool, opposite direction.
4. **America learns it was in a recession almost a year late** (rows 42–43). The Dec 2007 start was announced 1 Dec 2008, after a phone call on 28 Nov. The June 2009 end was announced in Sep 2010. The committee's chair in 2008 was Robert Hall.
5. **The two laureates shared a stage 25 years ago** (row 25). At Jackson Hole in 2001, Hall was the official discussant of Woodford's paper "Monetary Policy in the Information Economy".

**Best hook fact (my pick):** row 26 + 27. 「2011年8月9日，美联储一分钱没动，只加了一句"至少到2013年年中"，市场对未来两年利率的预期立刻掉到接近零。」 It works because it is literally *words only* (the rate was already zero) and it is backed by the laureate's own paper. Alternative with a bigger human pull: Draghi's 「相信我，这就够了」 (row 31) + OMT never used by 2015 (row 36).

**Strongest human story:** Hall at the 28 Nov 2008 conference call (row 42). Seven economists on a phone line decide, eleven months late, that the Great Recession began in Dec 2007. Families had been losing jobs for a year before anyone "officially" said so. It fits his prize work too (row 13): what people *expect* about income drives what they spend today.

**Viewer-life bridge (honest):** rows 5, 6 and 13 (the committee's own words). You save or spend based on what you expect tomorrow. Firms set prices the same way. So an announcement changes today. China anchor: rows 52–53. Your parents' floating mortgage = LPR + 加点, and the LPR is reset monthly on the 20th. Mechanics only.

**Best visual image:** a single line of text (the FOMC sentence, or 「相信我，这就够了」) dropping into a still pond, and a yield line (Spain 6.80 → 5.92 → 5.34) sliding down a staircase of months. For the twist, the 2013 line climbing 1.76 → 2.81 after a single sentence. The committee's "roller coaster of deep valleys and high peaks" (row 10) is an official metaphor the art director can use.
