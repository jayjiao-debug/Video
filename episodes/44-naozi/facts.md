# Facts table: 《爱因斯坦的大脑》 (ep44, v1)

Researcher, 2026-10-24. Every on-screen claim must come from a row here.

Status key:
- ✓ = verified in the source I opened.
- Disputed = the sources disagree. Use the safe wording given in the row.
- ✗ = false. Don't use it.
- 估算 = our own arithmetic.
- caution = usable only in the wording given.
- UNVERIFIED = do not put on screen.

Chinese lines marked 本片译 are our own translations of the verified English, German or Spanish. The suggested lines aim for ≤16 字.

**Corrections to the brief (read first)**
1. **"In a beer cooler" is a myth that grew in later retellings.** Levy found the brain in 1978. His own account: "Einstein's brain in a beer cooler?" — "No. He turned away from the cooler". The jars were in the bottom of two stacked boxes, "which had a logo reading COSTA CIDER on the side" (B19). Later retellings put it "under a beer cooler" (NPR 2005) or "in a beer cooler" (SciAm 2015). On screen, say 「苹果酒纸箱」.
2. **240 vs 170 pieces.** Harvey co-authored the 1999 Lancet paper, which says "approximately 240 blocks". Falk 2013 and Burrell 2015 agree. NMHM's finding aid says "170 pieces", and the 2012 NMHM/AFP coverage says "about 170 parts". 170 is also the number of blocks still at Princeton (Burrell). Use 「约240块」 (Harvey's own paper) or, safe for every source, 「上百块」 (B11).
3. **Permission is disputed, so don't say 偷.**
   - NMHM: "No permission for the removal and preservation had been given by Einstein or his family."
   - But the 1955 press said the removal was "decreed by the great scientist during his life" (JTA), and Levy 1978 wrote "Einstein had requested his brain be removed for posthumous study".
   - Hans Albert's consent came **afterwards**, on the condition that it be used only for science (B5–B6).
   - Safe line: 「事先没有家属同意」 + 「事后，儿子同意了：只许用于科学」.
4. **"23 years unknown" means unknown to the public.** In 1955 the papers said the brain would go to Dr. Harry Zimmerman in New York and be examined "within the next few days" (B8). After that, nothing appeared until Levy in Aug 1978. Say 「23年，外界不知道你在哪」.
5. **Harvey didn't lose his job right away.** He left Princeton Hospital in **1960**: Levy says he "left", while Burrell and Paterniti say he was fired. Only NPR 2005 says "within months" (B16).
6. **The drive was at the end of February 1997**, so the counter reads **117**, not 118. Harper's ran the story in **October 1997** (B22).
7. **Mütter Museum:** 46 slides arrived in Nov 2011 and went on show then. How many are on view in 2026 is UNVERIFIED. Since 2023 the museum doesn't show images of human remains online. Say 「收藏着」 rather than 「展出」 for today (B26).
8. **Nobel Prize:** the 1921 prize was held over and given in **1922**, for the photoelectric effect. His Nobel lecture (1923) was about relativity anyway (L11).
9. **The compass:** the father **showed** it to him, "at four or five" (L5), so say 「给他看」, not 「送给他」.
10. **"I have no special talents…":** the German wording is verified (Berlin memorial plaque). The recipient and date (Carl Seelig, 11 Mar 1952) come from a quotation book I could not open. Use it, but caption it 「——爱因斯坦」 without a date (L16). Einstein quotes are often fake (Nature 2018, Robinson: "Einstein said that — didn't he?" https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-018-05004-4/d41586-018-05004-4.pdf).

## Story-worthy

**Cold-open candidates, ranked:**
1. **The cider box (1978).** A reporter finds the brain of the world's most famous scientist in Wichita, in a box marked COSTA CIDER. Inside: "dozens of rectangular translucent blocks", "each with a little sticker reading CEREBRAL CORTEX and bearing a number" (B19–B20). Harvey had first said "I don't have any material here" (B18).
2. **84 years old, 4,000 miles, Tupperware in the trunk (1997).** Pieces of the brain rode in a duffel bag in a rented teal-green Buick to Einstein's granddaughter in Berkeley. She didn't take it (B22).
3. **Cut into about 240 numbered blocks, then into slides 0.02 mm thick** (B11, B14). Shown as glowing numbered cubes, this is the fastest image of "what happened to you".
4. **No grave, by choice.** His ashes were scattered secretly "to discourage idolaters", yet his brain sits in three institutions and his eyes went to his eye doctor (B8, B24–B28).
5. **"Perhaps a year away."** In 1955 the papers said study would begin "within the next few days" (B9). In 1978 Harvey said results were "perhaps a year away", and he kept saying it for decades (B21).
6. **Not heavier than anyone's.** 1230 g against a control average of 1400 g: "a large (heavy) brain is not a necessary condition for exceptional intellect" (B10).
7. **"Found it" × 3, "disputed" × 3.** More glia (1 of 4 blocks); parietal lobes 15% wider (no operculum, later contradicted); an extra fold (maybe the violin). At least 18 researchers, 6 papers, and "None has revealed a credible anatomical basis" (G1–G4).
8. **The Nobel wasn't for relativity**, and he lectured on relativity anyway (L11).

**Strongest human story:** Thomas Harvey. He was the 42-year-old on-call pathologist who removed the brain without the family's prior consent, believing "we were going to study the brain". He spent the rest of his life tied to it:
- In 1960 he left Princeton Hospital.
- He moved to Kansas, and in 1988 lost his medical licence after a three-day exam.
- For decades he kept saying the results were "a year away".
- At 84 he drove the brain across the country to give it to Einstein's granddaughter, who didn't take it.
- In 1998 he handed it back to the man in his old office at Princeton.
- He died in 2007 at 94.

The Mütter curator: "that brain was almost like a curse to him" (B16–B25). Tell it without villainy. The sources disagree on motive.

**Best visual image:** the numbered translucent blocks labelled CEREBRAL CORTEX, rising out of a cider box as glowing cubes, then reassembling into the brain's outline along Harvey's roadmap (P1–P3). For the bookend, a compass needle, which "had to have something deeply hidden behind it" (L5).

## Disputed and open items

- **Minute of death:** 1:15 (1955 press) vs 1:20 (Cervantes). Use 「凌晨1点多」.
- **Autopsy hour:** 8 a.m. (NMHM) vs "shortly before noon" (Daily Princetonian 1955) vs "within 7 hours" (Witelson). Use 「几个小时后」.
- **Permission:** no prior permission (NMHM, Inquirer, Burrell, PUP) vs "decreed by" Einstein (JTA 1955) / "had requested" (Levy) / "insisted" (Clark via Witelson). Harvey claimed Nathan's consent; Nathan denied it. Hans Albert's consent came afterwards (all sources).
- **Number of blocks:** ~240 (Witelson, Falk, Burrell) vs 170 (NMHM, 2012 coverage) vs "over/more than 200" (NPR, NMHM slide note).
- **Sectioning time:** 3 / 6 / 8 months. Slide sets: 12 (NMHM, Burrell) vs "5 to 12" (Falk).
- **Weight:** 1,197 g (Levy's 2.64 lb, 估算) / 1,224 g / 1,230 g. Use 「约1.2公斤」.
- **Firing:** 1960 (Levy "left"; Burrell 2015 and Paterniti "fired") vs "within months" (NPR 2005). Reasons vary.
- **Beer cooler:** ✗ in Levy's own account. Later retellings put it under or in a beer cooler.
- **Ashes:** "secretly" ✓; Delaware River = one secondary source.
- **Eyes:** Henry vs Harry Abrams; bank vault city unknown.

- **Mütter:** 46 slides received; the number on view in 2026 is UNVERIFIED.

## Risks

- **Human remains (platform).** No photos of the real brain, the slices or the slides. The Mütter itself stopped showing images of human remains online in 2023. Douyin may flag gore. Draw the brain as an outline or light, the blocks as glowing cubes and the slides as abstract light (brief already says so). No surgery or autopsy imagery: show a closed door or a lamp turning off.
- **Real people.**
  - **Harvey** (d. 2007): his family is alive. Avoid 偷 and 疯; use the disputed-permission wording (B5).
  - **Evelyn Einstein** (d. 2011): Paterniti mentions a question about her parentage. Don't touch it.
  - **Steven Levy, Michael Paterniti, Dean Falk, Sandra Witelson, Terence Hines** are living authors and researchers. Quote only their published words.
  - **Helen Dukas's** quotes come via secondary sources: say 「据他的秘书回忆」 if attributing.
- **Name and likeness.** Einstein's name and image are commercially licensed (rights reportedly held by the Hebrew University of Jerusalem; UNVERIFIED here). For an editorial film this is usually fine, but don't put his photo on the cover as a product image. A drawn silhouette is safer.
- **Nazi Germany 1933:** no swastikas or Nazi symbols (hard platform block).
- **Atomic bomb 1939:** no mushroom clouds or Hiroshima/Nagasaki casualty images. Keep it to the letter and his later regret, with the "some justification" half of the quote.
- **Israel 1952:** in 2026 any Israel beat can draw hostile comments. If kept, no flag, no map, one neutral line.
- **Maps:** the 1997 drive must **not** use a US map with borders (owner rule: no maps with borders). Show a road, an odometer and city names only (Wichita/Kansas → Berkeley).
- **Medical:** the aneurysm and the refusal of surgery are fine. Avoid language that glamorises refusing treatment. His words are about his own life at 76.
- **Fake quotes:** Einstein is the most misquoted scientist (Nature 2018). Use only L5, L13, L16 and B2, in the wording given.
