# ep37 孤独，会传染 — facts (researched 2026-10-22)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The Framingham Heart Study (Framingham, Massachusetts) has followed residents since **1948**, originally to study heart disease. Its tracking sheets (1971–2007) record who is whose friend, spouse, sibling, neighbour — a network of **12,067** people. The loneliness analysis used the Offspring Cohort (**5,124** people), asked at each exam (every 2–4 years): "how many days in the past week did you feel lonely?" (CES-D item). | Cacioppo, Fowler & Christakis 2009, *J Personality and Social Psychology* 97(6):977–991 [1][2] | ✓ |
| C2 | Loneliness occurs in **clusters** and extends up to **three degrees of separation**: a directly connected lonely person → **52%** more likely to be lonely; two degrees **25%**; three degrees **15%**; no effect at four. | [1] | ✓ |
| C3 | Spread is **stronger through friends than family** (siblings: no effect), stronger in **women** than men; immediate neighbours also matter. | [1][2] | ✓ |
| C4 | Lonely people sit disproportionately at the **periphery** and lose about **8% of their friends** by the next exam (~4 years later); "our social fabric can fray at the edges, like a yarn that comes loose at the end of a crocheted sweater." Authors suggest "aggressively targeting the people in the periphery to help repair their social networks." | [1][2] | ✓ quote |
| C5 | Each additional (non-lonely) friend reduced loneliness slightly (~0.04 days/week); lonely contacts increased it. Loneliness spread more strongly than perceived connection. | [1] | ✓ (no "2.5×" on screen — not double-checked) |
| B1 | 23 female students in fMRI: lonelier people showed **weaker ventral-striatum (reward) activity to pleasant pictures of people**, and weaker temporoparietal-junction (perspective-taking) activity to people in unpleasant situations. | Cacioppo, Norris, Decety, Monteleone & Nusbaum 2009, *J Cognitive Neuroscience* 21(1):83 [3] | ✓ |
| H1 | Meta-analysis of **148** studies, **308,849** people: stronger social relationships → **50%** higher likelihood of survival (OR 1.50); "comparable with well-established risk factors for mortality." | Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010, *PLoS Medicine* [4] | ✓ (not the contested "15 cigarettes" line) |
| I1 | Meta-analysis of loneliness interventions (20 randomized trials in the pooled analysis): four kinds — social skills, social support, more chances to meet, changing **social cognition** (how lonely people think about themselves and others). The last was "far more effective than the other strategies"; lonely people hold "incorrect assumptions about themselves and about how other people perceive them." | Masi, Chen, Hawkley & Cacioppo 2011, *Personality and Social Psychology Review* [5] | ✓ |
