# ep36 分手以后，为什么反而更放不下？ — facts (researched 2026-10-22)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Biological anthropologist Helen Fisher and colleagues scanned **15** people (10 women, 5 men) who had recently been rejected but were still intensely in love. Average **~2 months (63 days)** since the rejection; relationships had lasted ~2 years. They reported thinking about the rejecter **more than 85% of their waking hours**. | Fisher, Brown, Aron, Strong & Mashek 2010, *J Neurophysiology* 104:51–60 [1][2][3] | ✓ |
| F2 | Looking at the rejecter's photo (vs a familiar neutral person) activated the reward system (ventral tegmental area, ventral striatum) and regions linked to craving and addiction (nucleus accumbens, orbitofrontal/prefrontal cortex). Authors: "consistent with the hypothesis that romantic rejection is a specific form of addiction." | [1][2] | ✓ (say "和上瘾、渴求有关的脑区", not "就是毒瘾") |
| F3 | The more days since the rejection, the lower the activity in the right ventral putamen/pallidum, a region linked to attachment. (Rutgers notes "time heals" is the authors' speculation.) | [2] | ✓ phrase as "也许" |
| K1 | Kross et al.: **40** people with an unwanted break-up in the **past six months** who felt intensely rejected. In the scanner: ex's photo + think about the break-up vs a friend's photo + a recent good moment; and a thermal probe on the left forearm, painful ("like holding a very hot cup of coffee") vs warm. Rejection activated secondary somatosensory cortex and dorsal posterior insula — regions of physical pain sensation. | Kross, Berman, Mischel, Smith & Wager 2011, *PNAS* 108:6270–6275 [4][5] | ✓ (say "点亮了一部分相同的区域", not "同一块") |
| M1 | **464** people after a break-up: watching the ex's Facebook page ("surveillance") was linked to more distress, negative feelings, sexual desire and **longing** for the ex, and **lower personal growth**, controlling for offline contact, personality and relationship features. | Marshall 2012, *Cyberpsychology, Behavior, and Social Networking* 15(10):521–526 [6] | ✓ (correlational: "越…越…", not "导致") |
| P1 | Koban, Wager et al. 2017: **40** people with an unwanted break-up within six months; after the scans, half were given a nasal spray described as "a powerful analgesic effective in reducing emotional pain" — it was saline (the other half were told it was saline). The placebo group felt less physical pain and **felt better emotionally**; dorsolateral prefrontal activity rose and rejection-related activity quieted. | Koban et al. 2017, *J Neuroscience* [7] | ✓ |
| X1 | (not used) "It takes 11 weeks / half the relationship length to get over an ex" — not found in a primary source we could verify. | — | ✗ |
