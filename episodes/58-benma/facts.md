## Risks

Crowning one species would be a plain-reading mismatch (owner-notes row "只有我过得不好…误导"). Present the readings as 「有人说…」 and keep the swallow/hawk/crow/wind-god list together.
- **Real people**: 郭沫若 (don't frame it as "郭沫若错了", HK01's angle; the naming is disputed); 初世宾 (died 2025-03-02) and 宁强: respectful tone, no mockery of the 「马踏飞燕」 name; 党寿山 and the villagers: the "sell to buy horses" moment is human, not stupid. Show their reasoning (their plough horses had died).

## Most surprising, story-worthy facts
- **Human story**: in 1969 the villagers who broke into the tomb had just lost two plough horses and proposed selling the finds to buy horses. One county cultural official talked them out of it (row 3). Keep any "traded for two real horses" line as a framing, not a claim: the source says 「有村民提议」.
- **The bird is the stand**: three legs touch nothing; all 7 kg-plus rests on the right hind hoof on the bird, whose spread wings act as the base, with iron "bones" cast inside the bronze legs (rows 11, 13, 14).
- **Nobody knows what bird it is.** The museum's own name, 铜奔马, is a placeholder 「在无法确知其名的情况下」, and 人民日报 2026 says the species 「尚无定论」. The tail isn't forked like a swallow's; the head turns 180° like a hawk's (rows 23, 26, 27, 31).
- **Four rival readings**: swallow, hunting hawk (maybe with a bell hole in its tail), the sun crow, and 龙雀/飞廉, the wind god, which makes it 「神马快过风神」 (rows 25–30).
- **It walks 「顺拐」**: same-side legs together, a trained pacing gait that 走马 in Gansu still race today (rows 17, 19).
- **Best visual image**: the startled bird twisting its head back as the hoof lands on its back, 「飞鸟惊慌回首的刹那，骏马已疾奔而过」 (row 32), then a pull-back revealing the bird is the stand (row 13). Hook alternatives: the 1983 logo silhouette everyone has seen (row 34), the 2022 green plush (row 41) or the 2026 「吉祥马」 mascot (row 42).

### Middle section: "there are so many horse statues; why this one?" (round 2)
- **From ox carts to 300,000 horses**: at the start of the Han the emperor couldn't find four matching horses and ministers rode ox carts. Two generations later the state ran 36 pastures with 300,000 horses, because 匈奴 cavalry set the terms of war (rows 46, 48–50).
- **The war for horses**: 60,000+ men and 30,000+ horses marched on 大宛. They came back with a few dozen top horses and 3,000 ordinary ones; 10,000-odd men and 1,000-odd horses got back through 玉门关. A Han critic summed it up as 「厪獲駿馬三十匹」 (rows 51–52).
- **The empire had an official "perfect horse"**: 马援 cast a bronze model horse as the national standard, because 「马者甲兵之本，国之大用」. One scholar suspects the 铜奔马 is such a model, and reads the bird as the crow from the horse manual's 「三寸可以袭乌」 (rows 55–57).
- **The only one moving**: in a tomb full of bronze horses standing still, this is the only one in mid-gallop. The museum's former deputy director thinks it may not belong to the procession at all, perhaps a 「马神」 the general worshipped in life (rows 59, 60, 62).
- **Iron bones inside bronze**: cast in clay piece-moulds (not lost-wax) in sections, with a mortise-and-tenon iron bone in the leg. It works because iron stays solid at the temperature of molten bronze (rows 65–68).

**Thought-provoking, ranked**: (1) rows 59–60/62: the most famous member of the procession might not belong to it; (2) rows 51–52: a war that cost tens of thousands of men for a few dozen top horses; (3) rows 56–57: the bird may be the horse's "grade": fast enough to catch a crow.
