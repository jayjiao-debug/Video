## Story-worthy
1. Collingwood cut in "about the Twelfth from the Rear" — exactly the "twelfth Ship from their Rear" Nelson had written twelve days earlier (T2, T9).
2. The charge was slow: just over 2 knots, about walking pace, for most of a morning, straight into broadsides (T5, T6).
3. *Victory* lost about 20 killed and 30 wounded before firing a single gun (T7).
4. The enemy van got its signal to turn at 2:30 pm, two hours after the line was cut; the four that escaped were caught two weeks later (T11).
5. Of 19 prizes only 4 reached Gibraltar: the next day's storm took the rest (T14).
- Strongest human story: Nelson hit at 1:15 by a musket ball from *Redoutable*'s mizzen-top, 15 yards away, dying below decks at 4:30 while Hardy counted surrendering ships ("I bargained for twenty", Beatty [12]) (T4, T12).
- Best visual image: top-down at noon, a loose crescent bowed away from the wind, two thin files crawling down on it from the west over a long swell, then the two cut points (12th from rear, 10th from van) with the van still sailing north.

## Risks
- War dead (~6,000 killed): grey tallies, no gore; Nelson's death as a fact line only.
- Maps: Trafalgar is off Spain (Cádiz); keep any coastline generic, with no modern borders. Gibraltar's status is sensitive (UK/Spain), so don't label it on a map.
- Real people: Nelson, Collingwood, Villeneuve, Dumanoir, Lanchester, Jellicoe — quotes only as verified above. Don't call Dumanoir a coward (contested; he defended himself in print).
- Model stylisation: an all-"Bucentaure" Combined Fleet and an all-"Agamemnon" British fleet is fine as symbols; avoid on-screen ship names or gun counts on the generic models.
- Platform: war history with no modern politics; low risk.

## ep38 v2 examples (researched 2026-10-23)

Answer to the key question (ants): **no clean square law in ants.** The idea comes from a 1993 theory paper; the one direct casualty test (fire ants, 2005) fitted the **linear** law; a field test (Costa Rica, 2000) found numbers win in the open and size wins at a narrow entrance; a 2023 test found big meat ants beat Argentine ants even when outnumbered 10 to 1. Honest framing: 「拼数量还是拼个头，要看地形」. Chimps (2002) give the cleanest animal line: a square-law model predicts they fight only with 1.5× the numbers.

### Story-worthy (v2)
1. Meat ants: 20 against 200 Argentine ants, never beaten in a group battle (E5), a real case where size beats a 10-to-1 crowd. A perfect "but…" beat for the honest-limit line.
2. One knight beats 4 swordsmen and loses to 5 (E16): the square law's tipping point inside a game everyone knows, from a PNAS paper.
3. Chimpanzees join a fight only with 1.5× the numbers (E8, a model prediction).
4. Fire ants, the one direct casualty test, came out **linear** (θ = 1.04, E3): the law is about *how* you fight (everyone shooting at once), not about animals in general.
5. Japan turned the N² law into market-share targets: 41.7 % "to hold No.1 stably", about 74 % "overwhelming" (E12), and calls everyone but No.1 a 弱者 (E11).
- Strongest human story: 田岡信夫, a social-psychology researcher who in summer 1962 at a 高野山 temple seminar decided with his friend 斧田大公望 to study a WWI engineer's combat law, and turned it into Japan's best-known small-business playbook; he died at 57 (E9, E10).
- Best visual image: the same dots-and-squares from part 1, now as ants: 20 big dots vs 200 small dots in an open arena (the small ones swarm) and then in a maze of twigs (the big ones hold each gap). Same maths, different ground.
