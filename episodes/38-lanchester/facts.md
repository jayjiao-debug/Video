# ep38 兰彻斯特平方律 — facts (researched 2026-10-22)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Frederick W. Lanchester (British engineer) set out the "N-square law" in articles in *Engineering* (1914) and the book *Aircraft in Warfare: The Dawn of the Fourth Arm* (1916): ch. V "The N-Square Law", ch. VI "Nelson's Tactical Scheme — The N-Square Law at Trafalgar". | Lanchester 1916 [1] (contents, preface) | ✓ |
| L2 | Lanchester's contrast (§20): in old warfare "one man would ordinarily find himself opposed to one man"; under modern conditions "the concentration of superior numbers gives an immediate superiority in the active combatant ranks", and the smaller side takes more fire per man than it can return. | [1] §20 | ✓ |
| L3 | The law: with aimed fire, each side's losses are proportional to the other side's numbers; then b·B² − r·R² stays constant through the fight; fighting strength ∝ quality × number². | Chan, *Pointer* 42(2) [2]; MacKay et al. [3] | ✓ |
| L4 | Worked example (our arithmetic from L3): 1000 vs 500, equal quality → 1000² − 500² = 750,000 → winner keeps √750,000 ≈ **866** (loses ≈ 134). Round-by-round with 10 % per round: 1000/500 → 950/400 → 910/305 → 880/214 → 858/126 → 846/40 → ≈842/0; finer rounds → 866. | computed | ✓ (show as our calculation) |
| L5 | Divide and conquer (our arithmetic): 1000 vs 1000 → both annihilated. 1000 vs 500, then the 866 survivors vs the other 500 → √(866² − 500²) = √500,000 ≈ **707** left. Equivalent to doubling strength (N²), i.e. ≈1.41× the troops — not 2×. | computed from L3 | ✓ |
| L6 | To beat two equal opponents in a fair fight a ship needs to be **four times** as powerful. | The Conversation (MacKay) [3] | ✓ |
| L7 | Jellicoe wrote to Lanchester in 1916: "Your N-square law has become quite famous in the Grand Fleet." | [3] | ✓ |
| L8 | Real battles fit the square law only roughly: fitted exponent between 1 and 1.5 (Ardennes, Kursk, Iwo Jima studies); a "useful heuristic", not exact. | [2] citing Johnson 1990 | ✓ (one honest line) |
| T1 | Trafalgar, **21 Oct 1805**: British **27** ships of the line vs Franco-Spanish **33** ("Thirty-three ships, of which eighteen were French and fifteen Spanish" — Collingwood's despatch). | London Gazette 6 Nov 1805 [4]; [2] | ✓ |
| T2 | Nelson's memorandum, 9 Oct 1805: attack in two lines; the second line to "lead through, about their twelfth Ship from their Rear"; "no Captain can do very wrong if he places his Ship alongside that of an Enemy." | Memorandum text [5]; RMG archive copy [6] | ✓ |
| T3 | On the day: two columns sailed straight at the enemy line (Nelson at the centre, Collingwood at the rear), cut it and split it into groups (Chan: 13 / 5 / 15); the van could not get back into the fight in time and finally withdrew. | [2]; [7] | ✓ (no clock times) |
| T4 | Result: Britain lost **no ship**; the Combined Fleet lost **18–19** of 33 (Gazette: 19). Nelson was shot by a musketeer from *Redoutable* and died that afternoon. | [4]; [7] | ✓ ("一半以上") |
| S1 | 孙子·虚实篇: "我专而敌分，我专为一，敌分为十，是以十攻其一也。则我众而敌寡。" | ctext [8] | ✓ |
