| L3 | The law: with aimed fire, each side's losses are proportional to the other side's numbers; then b·B² − r·R² stays constant through the fight; fighting strength ∝ quality × number². | Chan, *Pointer* 42(2) [2]; MacKay et al. [3] | ✓ |

## Story-worthy
1. Collingwood cut in "about the Twelfth from the Rear" — exactly the "twelfth Ship from their Rear" Nelson had written twelve days earlier (T2, T9).
2. The charge was slow: just over 2 knots, about walking pace, for most of a morning, straight into broadsides (T5, T6).
3. *Victory* lost about 20 killed and 30 wounded before firing a single gun (T7).
4. The enemy van got its signal to turn at 2:30 pm, two hours after the line was cut; the four that escaped were caught two weeks later (T11).
5. Of 19 prizes only 4 reached Gibraltar: the next day's storm took the rest (T14).
- Strongest human story: Nelson hit at 1:15 by a musket ball from *Redoutable*'s mizzen-top, 15 yards away, dying below decks at 4:30 while Hardy counted surrendering ships ("I bargained for twenty", Beatty [12]) (T4, T12).
- Best visual image: top-down at noon, a loose crescent bowed away from the wind, two thin files crawling down on it from the west over a long swell, then the two cut points (12th from rear, 10th from van) with the van still sailing north.

## Risks
- War dead (~6,000 killed): grey tallies, no gore; Nelson's death as a fact line only.
- Maps: Trafalgar is off Spain (Cádiz); keep any coastline generic, with no modern borders. Gibraltar's status is sensitive (UK/Spain), so don't label it on a map.
- Real people: Nelson, Collingwood, Villeneuve, Dumanoir, Lanchester, Jellicoe — quotes only as verified above. Don't call Dumanoir a coward (contested; he defended himself in print).
- Model stylisation: an all-"Bucentaure" Combined Fleet and an all-"Agamemnon" British fleet is fine as symbols; avoid on-screen ship names or gun counts on the generic models.
- Platform: war history with no modern politics; low risk.
