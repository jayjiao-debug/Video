# ep35 推石头的人，是幸福的 — facts (researched 2026-10-20)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Camus began *Le Mythe de Sisyphe* in 1940 (the Fall of France) and Gallimard published it in 1942; the essay starts from whether the absurd demands suicide and ends "Il faut imaginer Sisyphe heureux" (One must imagine Sisyphus happy). Camus was born 7 Nov 1913 → **28** at publication (Oct 1942). | Wikipedia summary [1]; text [2] | ✓ |
| C2 | Camus: the gods "had thought with some reason that there is no more dreadful punishment than futile and hopeless labor"; "It is during that return, that pause, that Sisyphus interests me"; "The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart." | Camus, *The Myth of Sisyphus* (O'Brien tr.) [2][3] | ✓ quotes |
| C3 | The myth: Sisyphus pushes a stone up a hill and it rolls back down (Homer, *Odyssey* 11). | Homer; [3] | ✓ standard |
| L1 | Lego/Bionicle experiment: paid $2.00 for the first model, 11¢ less each next one ($0.02 from the 20th). **Meaningful** (models kept on the desk, N=20) vs **Sisyphus** (the experimenter took each finished model apart in front of the subject, N=20). Built **10.6 vs 7.2** on average; earned $14.40 vs $11.52. | Ariely, Kamenica & Prelec 2008, *J Economic Behavior & Organization* 67:671–677 — manuscript [4] | ✓ (note: Ariely has had unrelated data-integrity controversies; this study is not among them, co-authored with Kamenica & Prelec) |
| W1 | 2,250 volunteers (18–88) sampled at random moments via an iPhone app: minds wandered **46.9%** of waking hours; mind-wandering predicted unhappiness better than the activity itself; time-lag analysis suggests wandering causes unhappiness. "A wandering mind is an unhappy mind." | Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010, *Science* 330:932 — Harvard Gazette [5] | ✓ |
| J1 | 196 employees (135 classifiable): **job 33% · career 32% · calling 36%**; among 24 administrative assistants (same job): 9 / 7 / 8. Calling → higher life and job satisfaction; days missed 2.0 vs 3.2 (job) vs 4.3 (career). | Wrzesniewski, McCauley, Rozin & Schwartz 1997, *J Research in Personality* 31:21–33 [6] | ✓ paper |
| X1 | (not used) Brickman 1978 lottery winners 4.00 vs controls 3.82 — small samples; later large Swedish lottery data (Lindqvist et al. 2020) found lasting gains in life satisfaction → dropped. | [7] | ✗ dropped |
