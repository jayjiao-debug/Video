**Corrections to brief_v3 (read first)**
1. Bell: Watson's own 1913 memory of the sentence is "Mr. Watson, come here, I want you." The notebook written that night says "…I want to see you." Keep 我想见你 and attribute it to Bell's notebook (B1).
2. Bell: both men were in two rooms on the **same top floor** at 5 Exeter Place, and the doors were shut (B4). Museums Victoria says "basement… three flights of stairs", but the two primary sources contradict it, so don't use it. Don't show acid being spilled either: neither the notebook nor Watson 1913 mentions it (B4).
3. Marconi: Poldhu didn't send S once. It sent "a succession of S's" every 10 minutes, with 5-minute rests, during set hours each day (M2). On 12 Dec Poldhu wasn't the famous ring of 20 masts. A storm wrecked that on 17 Sep 1901, and a fan of 60 wires hung between two masts replaced it (P-M4).
4. ARPANET: sources disagree on the time of "LO", so use 「那天晚上」 rather than 22:30 (A1). Distance sources range from 350 to 400 miles (A4).
5. 1987 email: 「中国第一封电子邮件」 is not safe: CNNIC records a 1986 remote-login email (E6). Nobody verified typed it. Thirteen Chinese and German scientists signed it. 钱天白 is not among them (E4). It was sent on Sunday 20 Sep at 23:55 Beijing time, six days late, because the mail software deadlocked (E1–E2).
6. 1977: Druyan's own words describe brain waves plus "every little sound that my body was making", so don't say only "heartbeat". The recording was made two days after the call (V3).
7. Voyager 1 reaches exactly one light-day from Earth on **18 Nov 2026** (NASA). That's the finale's fact (V7).

## Story-worthy

1. **The first reply on a telephone was a question.** After "come here", the roles were swapped, and the one sentence that came through clearly was Watson's "Mr. Bell, do you understand what I say?" (B3).
2. **Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth on 18 Nov 2026**, a few weeks after this film comes out. No human-made object has been that far. A sentence from Earth now takes a whole day to catch up with the love song it carries (V7).
3. **China's first email sat stuck for six days.** It was written on Monday 14 Sep 1987 and finally went out at 23:55 on Sunday 20 Sep after a software deadlock was fixed (E1, E2).
4. **The first "recording" of a human voice was never meant to be heard.** Scott drew sound as a soot line. It was heard 148 years later, and the "girl" turned out to be a man, probably Scott himself (S2, S3).
5. **Marconi's "S" wasn't one message but a heartbeat.** Cornwall sent S every ten minutes for hours, waiting for someone 3,000+ km away to catch it with a wire on a kite (M2).
- Bonus (Shang): a real verification reads 「昃，允雨自西」, "in the afternoon it did rain, from the west", which answers the four-directions question (SH3, caution: thesis source).
- Bonus (Tang): the excavated Tang beacon tower at 克亚克库都克 held **soldiers' letters home**, so the beacons and the letters really were in the same place (P-DF2).

**Strongest human story:** Ann Druyan, 1 June 1977. She found a Chinese guqin piece for the record and left a message at Carl Sagan's Tucson hotel: "Annie called." He rang back an hour later, and the call ended in a proposal ("for keeps?" / "you mean get married?" / "yes"). Two days later her brain waves, recorded while she thought about being in love, went onto the Golden Record. That record is now nearly one light-day away (V1–V3, V7).

**Best visual image:** one sentence travelling through matter. A crack jumps across a turtle shell from a glowing bronze rod (P-SH2). Then a single wire runs between two shut doors on a boarding-house top floor (P-B1). Then a linen kite strains in a Newfoundland gale, its wire running down into an abandoned hospital (P-M1/M2). Finally a 30 cm gold disc is bolted to the outside of a ten-sided box, a light-day from home (P-V2).

## Risks

- **Real people and quotes.** Druyan is alive. Her words come from NPR and Radiolab interviews, so quote them as said and don't invent dialogue. Kline and Duvall were private individuals at the time. Their names are in Kleinrock's and NPR's public accounts, which is fine, but don't add personal details. In the 1987 scene, don't name 钱天白 as the sender, because that credit is disputed and not supported by Zorn's paper. Use 「中德科学家」 instead.
- **Disputed facts.** Bell's exact sentence: follow the notebook and attribute it. The ARPANET time: say "evening". The kite type: don't name it. 合集12870: don't show the number. The 杜甫《述怀》 lines are unverified. The Golden Record Chinese characters are a transcription and NASA gives only the English.
- **Maps and borders.** If a map shows Beijing → Karlsruhe or the Atlantic, keep it to a simple great-circle line without borders. Any map of China must be the complete official map (standard map service, 审图号) or avoid outlines entirely. Safest: no country outlines.
- **War.** 安史之乱 / occupied Chang'an: show no killing, just fires on the horizon and an empty spring street.
- **Religion.** "What hath God wrought" is a Bible quote (Numbers 23:23), fine as historical text.
- **Nuclear.** Voyager's RTGs are plutonium power sources. Draw them as small finned cylinders on a boom and don't label them "nuclear".
- **Platform and licences.** The Scott audio is CC BY, so credit First Sounds. NASA audio and images are public domain (credit NASA/JPL-Caltech). For the Golden Record's 《流水》 audio, check the rights before using the actual recording; NASA's listing doesn't settle the rights to Guan Pinghu's performance. Lyrics: 「Au clair de la lune」 is a traditional pre-1929 song, so it's fine.
- **"First" claims.** Don't say "人类第一封电子邮件" or "中国第一封电子邮件" without hedging: Zorn's own mail body calls it the first mail sent from China via computer interconnection into the international scientific networks (paraphrase of the German PDF). Say 「1987年，北京发往德国的一封电子邮件」 or 「中国第一个电子邮件节点发出的邮件」 (see E6). ARPANET is not "the first network ever" (NPL existed), so say 「阿帕网上的第一条消息」.
- **Sources opened but blocked or partial** (for the record): Cambridge *Early China* PDFs (permission timeout); Smithsonian si.edu object pages for the Model 500 and the 3D key (site error); NPR 2009 page on the Scott re-speed (robots).
