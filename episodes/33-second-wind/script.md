# Script: 越熬越精神 (ep33) · first draft (producer, no crew yet)

**Logline:** You're sleepy at 8 p.m., and wide awake at 11. That isn't you "getting into the zone": in the hours before your usual bedtime your body clock pushes its wake signal hardest, a stretch sleep researchers call the "forbidden zone for sleep". It is stronger in teenagers than in adults, and the phone you pick up to fill it pushes the whole clock later.
**Takeaway:** 不是你不困，是生物钟在帮你硬撑。它松手的时候，别错过。
**Length:** ~100 s · 32 lines, all ≤ 16 字 · 1920×1080, subtitle-only, ~2 s Juno end card.
**End-card question:** 你几点最精神？评论区报个时间

## Title options
1. **越熬越精神** (5 字): the everyday phrase everyone says; the film explains it.
2. **睡眠禁区** (4 字): the real scientific name; mysterious, short, a strong cover.
3. **为什么一到晚上就精神？** (11 字): the hook as a question.

## Picture idea (one night, one take)
One bedroom at night, one continuous camera that drifts around it as the clock runs from 8 p.m. to 1 a.m. Two forces are made physical in the room: **sleep pressure** as a tide of dark water rising up the walls since morning, and the **body clock's wake signal** as a warm light (a lamp / a sunlike glow) that holds the tide back and is brightest just before it lets go. The "forbidden zone" is the moment the light flares and the tide can't reach the bed; the "sleep gate" is the light switching off and the tide rushing in. The phone is a cold blue light that drags the clock's hands later.

## Facts (first pass, producer; to be confirmed by the researcher)
| # | Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Lavie's ultrashort sleep-wake schedule (7 min sleep opportunity / 13 min awake, every 20 min, for ~24–36 h, with and without prior sleep deprivation): sleep propensity dropped markedly in the hours just before the nocturnal "sleep gate"; people could not fall asleep when told to. He named it the "forbidden zone for sleep"; sleep propensity then rises abruptly at the "sleep gate". | Lavie P (1986) *Electroencephalogr Clin Neurophysiol* 63:414–425, doi:10.1016/0013-4694(86)90123-9; described by Lavie in *Sleep Advances* 2024, PMC11100432 | read (Lavie's own account) |
| F2 | The forbidden zone (wake-maintenance zone) falls in the 2–3 hours before habitual bedtime, around melatonin onset. In a lab study, adolescents (14–17) took longer to fall asleep in it than adults (2 h before melatonin onset: 19.2 vs 9.2 min; 2 h after: 20.9 vs 7.0 min). | Monterastelli, Adams, Eastman & Crowley (2024) *Frontiers in Sleep* 2:1304647, doi:10.3389/frsle.2023.1304647 | paper read. **Note:** the study was 14–17-year-olds, so lines 22–24 say 十几岁 / 青少年, not 年轻人 |
| F3 | Two forces regulate sleep: sleep pressure that builds the longer you're awake, and a circadian (body-clock) process. | Borbély (1982) two-process model; Edgar et al. 1993 | standard model; cite Borbély 1982 |
| F4 | Squirrel monkeys with the body clock (SCN) removed: wake bouts fell from 223 ± 10 min to 15 ± 6 min; total sleep +4.0 h. The SCN actively promotes wakefulness against sleep pressure. | Edgar, Dement & Fuller (1993) *J Neurosci* 13(3):1065–1079, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.13-03-01065.1993 | abstract read.
