# Brief: 为什么一到深夜就emo

- **Episode:** ep52 · **Series:** VIBE知识大赏 (Juno)
- **Idea in one sentence:** Late at night your brain is not "you overthinking" — sleep loss and the
  circadian dip measurably shift mood, amplify rumination and weaken self-control (the "mind after
  midnight" research), and large-scale data on real posts/messages shows people's own words get
  darker the later it gets.
- **Why a 18–25 viewer cares:** 共鸣 topic. Everyone in this age group has lain awake at 2am
  spiraling about an ex, a mistake, their future — and wondered if something is wrong with them.
  Hook: 凌晨2点，你又开始想那个人了.
- **Takeaway (one line the viewer should remember):** 别在深夜做决定 — what you feel at 2am is your
  tired brain, not the truth; the same thought will look different at 10am.
- **Length target:** 80–100 s · **Format:** 1920×1080, subtitle-only, music (+ sound design if enabled)
- **Visual direction:** Juno look / free · references: none yet — art director proposes 2–3 looks
  after gate 1.
- **Must include / must avoid:**
  - Must: primary research only (peer-reviewed studies / large-scale primary datasets), not
    Wikipedia/百度百科/知乎 summaries. Cover: circadian mood rhythms, sleep loss and emotional
    regulation/amygdala reactivity, "mind after midnight" (elevated negative affect, impulsivity,
    suicidal ideation risk late at night independent of how long you've been awake), large-scale
    analyses of late-night social posts / messages skewing more negative/emotional.
  - Must: rate how solid each claim is in facts.md (RCT / large cohort / correlational / small-n /
    popular-but-unproven) and mark weak or popular-but-unproven claims UNVERIFIED (e.g. any specific
    "emo hour" time window, any precise % figures from listicles rather than papers).
  - Must: look for one concrete "about you" number every few lines (e.g. % of people who report X,
    how many minutes/hours sleep loss needed to shift mood, how much later negative word use rises).
  - Must: end comforting and practical — a concrete thing to do (e.g. 别在深夜做决定 / write it down,
    revisit in daylight) grounded in what the research actually supports, not a vague "it gets
    better."
  - Avoid: medicalising normal sadness as a disorder; avoid implying everyone who feels bad at night
    has a clinical problem; avoid stating any number the source doesn't actually give.
