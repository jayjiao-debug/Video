# ep34 七年后，你还是你吗？ — facts (researched 2026-10-20)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Cold-War atmospheric bomb tests (1950s–60s) made atmospheric ¹⁴C spike, peaking mid-1960s and falling since (effective half-life ~11 years after 1963). DNA locks in the ¹⁴C level of the year the cell was born and does not change → cells can be dated to about ±2 years. | Spalding, Bhardwaj, Buchholz, Druid & Frisén 2005, *Cell* 122:133–143 — Alzforum summary [1] | ✓ (size of the spike: sources differ — script says only 猛增) |
| S2 | Cortical neurons are **as old as the person** ("always as old as the individual donor"); total cortex DNA is younger (other cells turn over). | [1]; Times Higher Education [2] | ✓ |
| S3 | Intestinal cells (excluding the surface lining) average **15.9 years**; the gut-surface cells last about **5 days**; rib (intercostal) muscle cells average **15.1 years**. Frisén's tentative guess: the average adult cell may be **7–10 years** old. | THE [2] | ✓ |
| S4 | About **330 billion** cells are replaced daily (~1% of all cells); in **80–100 days** about **30 trillion** are replenished; blood and gut make up the great majority of turnover. (≈3.8 million per second, our arithmetic.) | Sender & Milo 2021, *Nature Medicine* 27 — Scientific American [3] | ✓ |
| S5 | Heart muscle cells renew ~**1%/year at 25**, falling to **0.45%/year at 75**; about **half** of the heart cells you are born with are replaced over a lifetime. | Bergmann et al. 2009, *Science* 324:98–102 — ScienceDaily [4] | ✓ |
| S6 | Lens: crystallin proteins are built from conception to about age 1–2, then "remain essentially unchanged for the rest of our lives". | Lynnerup et al. 2008, *PLoS ONE* — ScienceDaily [5] | ✓ |
| S7 | Fat cells: total number stays stable even after major weight loss (cells shrink); ~10% renewed per year. | Spalding et al. 2008, *Nature* 453:783 — Medical News Today [6] | ✓ number stable; 10%/yr from the abstract (to recheck if used) |
| P1 | 1947 Scottish Mental Survey: teachers rated **1,208** 14-year-olds on 6 traits (self-confidence, perseverance, stability of moods, conscientiousness, originality, desire to excel). In **2012**, 635 traced, **174** took part at **~77**; simple analysis: **no significant stability** for any trait over **63 years** ("as if from different people" — press); a rater-corrected model kept some stability for mood stability (and near-significant conscientiousness). Conclusion: "lifelong differential stability of personality is generally quite low". | Harris, Brett, Johnson & Deary 2016, *Psychology and Aging* 31(8):862–874 [7][8] | ✓ |
| P2 | End of history illusion: **19,000+** people aged **18–68**; at every age people say they changed a lot in the past decade but expect little change in the next. They'd pay **$80** to see their favourite band of 10 years ago today, but **$129** to see today's favourite band in 10 years. Gilbert: "our future selves will look back and think the very same thing about us." | Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson 2013, *Science* 339:96–98 — NYT [9]; summary [10] | ✓ |
| H1 | Ship of Theseus: Plutarch (*Life of Theseus*, ~100 AD) — the Athenians kept the ship, replacing rotten planks, and philosophers argued whether it was the same ship. Hobbes (*De Corpore*, 1655) added: rebuild a second ship from the old planks — which is the original? | Plutarch; Hobbes [11] | ✓ standard |
