# Arthur short: the English version

The same picture as the Chinese film, frame for frame (same cuts, same actions, 1991 frames), with every word in it in
English: narration, captions, corner tags, name tags, stamps, the pull counter and the letters on the stone.

Render it with the input prop `lang`:

```
npx remotion render src/index.ts ArthurFilm out/arthur_en.mp4 --props='{"lang":"en"}'
```

Without the prop the film is the Chinese one, unchanged.

## Where the English lives

- `src/videos/arthur/lang.ts`: the switch (`EN`, `L(zh, en)`) and every on-screen string (`TXT`). `TAG_X = 96`: the
  corner tags start 96 px from the left edge in both languages (phones that are taller than 9:16 cut about 50 px off
  each side).
- `src/videos/arthur/captions_en.ts`: GENERATED by `gen_en.py`. The English captions (short chunks, 72 px, set smaller
  when a chunk would be wider than 730 px, so that the app's button column does not cover its end) and the characters' quotes for the mouths (start, end, syllables).
- The shot files (`shots/seg*.ts`), `overlay.tsx` and `props.ts` read `EN` / `TXT` where the English type needs another
  place or size (English words are wider than two Chinese characters).

## The script

`script_en.json`: 21 lines, the same ones as the Chinese narration. Every line is split into groups, one English group
for each Chinese caption group (70 in all, `src/videos/arthur/captions.ts`), because the picture's actions sit on the
frames of those groups. A group has the spoken words (`t`), the caption chunks they are shown as (`cap`, 【】 = gold),
and for a character's own words who says them and how many syllables (`who`, `syl`: the mouth opens once a syllable).

The English is written to the time each group has, not translated word for word. Facts are the Chinese film's
(`facts.md` in the project files: Malory, Le Morte d'Arthur, Book 1, chapters 5 to 7). The letters on the stone are a
shortened form of Malory's "Whoso pulleth out this sword of this stone and anvil, is rightwise king born of all
England".

## Fitting the narration to the picture

1. Voice farm: one clip per line and take, named `x<nn><take>.mp3`, with `"words": true` in the request so that the
   report carries where Whisper heard every word.
2. `fit_en.py <clips dir> <report.json> <narration.wav> <en_timing.json> [line=take ...] [--takes ab]`
   For every line: pauses longer than 0.10 s are cut to 0.07 s (as for the Chinese takes), the take is cut at the group
   boundaries, and every group gets a tempo (`atempo`) and, where the take had a pause, a little more pause, so that
   each group starts near the first frame of its Chinese window and the line ends inside its slot
   (`../voice/voice_timing_v1.json`, the timing the picture was rendered with). The tempos of one line are held close
   together, because a jump in speed inside a sentence is heard and a few frames of offset are not; the Chinese
   narration sits about 3 frames off its caption boundaries too. Of several takes the one that fits with the smaller
   cost is used, unless a take is named (`13=c`). It prints the tempos, the offsets and what was not heard.
3. `gen_en.py <en_timing.json>` writes `captions_en.ts`: every caption chunk appears on its first word. Without a
   timing file it spreads the chunks over the Chinese windows (enough for stills).
4. Sound and delivery as for the Chinese film (`../sound/`): `sfx_mix.py` with the English narration and the same
   `cues.json` (the effects sit on picture frames, so they do not move), then `remux.sh`.

Nothing here keeps audio or video in the repository.
