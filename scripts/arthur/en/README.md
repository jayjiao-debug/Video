# Arthur short: the English version

The same picture as the Chinese film, frame for frame (same cuts, same actions, 1991 frames), with every word in it in
English: narration, captions, corner tags, name tags, stamps, the pull counter and the letters on the stone.

Render it with the input prop `lang`:

```
npx remotion render src/index.ts ArthurFilm out/arthur_en.mp4 --props='{"lang":"en"}'
```

Without the prop the film is the Chinese one, unchanged.

## Where the English lives

- `src/videos/arthur/lang.ts`: the switch (`EN`, `L(zh, en)`) and every on-screen string (`TXT`). `TAG_X`: the English
  corner tags start 96 px from the left edge (phones that are taller than 9:16 cut about 50 px off each side); the
  Chinese ones stay at 44 px, as published.
- `src/videos/arthur/captions_en.ts`: GENERATED by `gen_en.py`. The English captions (short chunks, 72 px, set smaller
  when a chunk would be wider than 730 px, so that the app's button column does not cover its end) and the characters' quotes for the mouths (start, end, syllables).
- The shot files (`shots/seg*.ts`), `overlay.tsx` and `props.ts` read `EN` / `TXT` where the English type needs another
  place or size (English words are wider than two Chinese characters).

## The script

`script_en.json`: 21 lines, the same ones as the Chinese narration. Every line is split into groups, one English group
for each Chinese caption group (70 in all, `src/videos/arthur/captions.ts`), because the picture's actions sit on the
frames of those groups. A group has the spoken words (`t`), the caption chunks they are shown as (`cap`, 【】 = gold),
and for a character's own words who says them and how many syllables (`who`, `syl`: the mouth opens once a syllable).

The English is written to the time each group has, not translated word for word. Facts are the Chinese film's
(`facts.md` in the project files: Malory, Le Morte d'Arthur, Book 1, chapters 5 to 7). The letters on the stone are a
shortened form of Malory's "Whoso pulleth out this sword of this stone and anvil, is rightwise king born of all
England".

## Fitting the narration to the picture

1. Voice farm: one clip per line and take, named `x<nn><take>.mp3`, with `"words": true` in the request so that the
   report carries where Whisper heard every word. A take letter that `script_en.json` lists under a line's `alt` is
   another wording of that line (shorter ones were written for the lines that needed the most speed-up).
2. `fit_en.py <clips dir> <report.json> <narration.wav> <en_timing.json> [line=take ...] [--takes abc] [--all 1]`
   For every line: pauses longer than 0.10 s are cut to 0.07 s (as for the Chinese takes) and the breath after the
   last word is cut off; the take is cut at the group boundaries (in a pause, or in a dip between two words; where the
   voice does not drop the two groups stay in one piece); every group gets a tempo (`atempo`) and, where there is a
   pause or a dip, a little more pause, so that each group starts near the first frame of its Chinese window and the
   line ends inside its slot (`../voice/voice_timing_v1.json`, the timing the picture was rendered with). The tempos
   of one line are held close together, because a jump in speed inside a sentence is heard and a few frames of offset
   are not (the Chinese narration sits about 3 frames off its caption boundaries too); the groups a stamp or the pull
   counter lands on ("Stuck", "Pull two") count three times. Of several takes the one with the smaller score is used
   (fit, plus a charge for speed-up), unless a take is named (`13=e`). It prints the tempos, the offsets, what was
   not heard and the boundaries it did not cut.
   Before it writes the track it levels the pieces (the voice shouts the characters' lines and drops on asides: each
   piece moves 0.6 of the way to the median level, one fixed gain per piece) and turns the phase (four all-pass
   filters at 200 Hz: no level changes at any frequency, the voice's tall one-sided peaks come down).
3. `gen_en.py <en_timing.json>` writes `captions_en.ts`: every caption chunk appears on its first word, in the wording
   of the take that was used. Without a timing file it spreads the chunks over the Chinese windows (enough for stills).
4. Sound and delivery as for the Chinese film (`../sound/`): `sfx_mix.py` with the English narration and the same
   `cues.json` (the effects sit on picture frames, so they do not move), then `remux.sh`.

The narration in use: the ElevenLabs library voice Alex (the owner's choice of four), model and settings as for the
Chinese narration, requests `arthur-en-alex-01` (two takes a line and shorter wordings of six lines), `-02`, `-03`
(more wordings of lines 4, 10, 13, 14, 17). `en_timing_alex.json` is what the fit wrote:

```
python3 fit_en.py <clips> <report.json> narration_en.wav en_timing_alex.json --takes abcdef 5=a 13=e
python3 gen_en.py en_timing_alex.json
python3 ../sound/sfx_mix.py narration_en.wav ../sound/cues.json <folder with the effect clips> out --dur 66.3667 --lufs -15.0
```

Tempo 1.12 on average (1.00 to 1.28; the Chinese narration needs 1.19). The groups start 1.8 frames from their
windows on average, 6 at most. Loudness: this voice has taller peaks than the Chinese one at the same loudness, so the
mix is set to -15.0 LUFS mono (-12 as a stereo file, half a decibel under the Chinese film): there the limiter works
exactly as much as it does on the Chinese narration at -14.5 (it turns down by more than 3 dB in 12.7 % of the 50 ms
windows with speech in both).
