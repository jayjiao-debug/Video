# Facts and sources

Why: Douyin's fact-check team (抖音小安) asked for proof on a published episode, and Wikipedia links don't convince
a platform reviewer because anyone can edit them. The owner's rule: credible sources only.

## Allowed as the cited source
- Official inquiries and reports, government publications
- National archives and museums
- Peer-reviewed papers with a DOI
- UN and intergovernmental agencies
- The researchers' own publication for their own data
- Established news organisations (Reuters, AP, BBC, NHK, 新华社) for what happened at an event

## Never cited
Wikipedia, 百度百科, 知乎 answers, personal blogs, content farms, AI-generated summaries, quote sites. They may point
to a lead; follow it to the primary source and cite that.

## The facts table (`facts.md`)
- One row per on-screen claim: the claim as shown, the exact figure or wording in the source, the source (title,
  publisher, year, URL/DOI you actually opened), and a note.
- Estimates are marked 估算; anything not verified is UNVERIFIED and may not be used in the script.
- When authoritative sources disagree, say which one is used and phrase the claim so it holds for all of them
  ("400多个空座位").
- If a number exists only in news reports, say so, so the answer is ready if a platform asks.
- Read primary accounts: retellings get details wrong.
- Schematic visuals are labelled 示意; contested explanations are hedged.
- The table feeds the end-card sources, the 简介 or pinned comment, and any platform reply.

## Platform fact-check reply (抖音小安)
Two messages: (1) the content is real events and published research, visuals are code-built 示意 animation
declared 内容由AI生成, then each claim with its number; (2) the source list with links. No original footage exists.
Don't edit the title or delete and re-post the video.
